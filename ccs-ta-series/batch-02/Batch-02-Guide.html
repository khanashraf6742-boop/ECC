How to use this batch

  1. Identify official purpose, civilian/service status, pay level, exact date, home posting, place and method of travel, and who pays. Obtain the competent sanction, clearances and funds.
  2. Apply the correct mode/class and relevant booking instructions. Distinguish international air, sea/steamer class, inter-city road mileage, domestic city travel, foreign duty conveyance and transfer of effects.
  3. For a foreign tour, verify the current MEA order, country rate, employee category, trip-duration slab, hotel panel, any permitted reductions and funding; do not transplant domestic tour DA.
  4. Keep approvals, itinerary, tickets and evidence, any required declarations, and the department's current rules. See the coverage ledger before treating a topic as fully checked.

Each numbered unit below includes an application example. A calculated ceiling is not a promise of payment.

A. Scope, dates and international air class

01 — Which rules and date?

The 2017 DoE order applies its revised civilian TA entitlements to journeys from 1 July 2017; earlier journeys start with the earlier orders. Its mode/class saving clause can preserve an employee or group’s existing higher entitlement until a higher entitlement arises normally. Basic pay in the appropriate level, not NPA, MSP or special pay, determines the relevant pay-level framework. International rules have their own table. S1

Example: A Level 13A civilian on a sanctioned foreign tour in 2026 starts with the international table and any applicable protection; a 2016 journey needs its then-applicable rules.

03 — Non-availability and rail-related bus provisions

The 2017 domestic travel conditions say that, if seats in an entitled class are unavailable, a Government servant may travel in the class below the entitled class. In the specified no-rail and rail-connected road situations, the separate bus/public-transport conditions in units 48–50 apply. This wording does not create an automatic right to a higher class. S1

Example: If an entitled rail class is unavailable, an employee may consider an available lower class subject to the applicable instructions; unavailable seats do not themselves authorise a higher fare class.

B. Booking air tickets on Government account

The 16 February 2022 and 16 June 2022 DoE instructions below should be read together and checked against later orders and the facts. These are Government-account instructions; do not assume their funding condition is identical for every third-party-funded visit. S7

04 — Airline and the three authorised agents

For air travel where Government of India bears the air-passage cost, the 2022 instructions require ticket purchase through Balmer Lawrie & Company Limited (BLCL), Ashok Travels & Tours (ATT), or IRCTC. Private airlines may be used for domestic and international Government-account air travel. Airline eligibility and booking channel are different questions. S7

Example: An approved foreign tour can use a private airline but, when the Government bears the fare, the ticket is booked through an authorised agent, subject to current instructions.

05 — Agent choice, group and charges

The Ministry/Department, or the official making a permitted self-booking, may choose among the three agents based on convenience and service quality. Tickets for all employees for a single tour should be booked through one selected agent. No agency charges/convenience fees are to be paid to the three agents. S8 (para 2)

Example: For four employees on the same tour, the office selects one of the three agents for the group rather than assuming each employee may select a different agent for that tour.

06 — Best available fare and the three-hour slot

Choose the cheapest fare available in the entitled travel class, preferably a non-stop flight, in the chosen three-hour slot on the travel day. The order permits optimisation within a 10% price band for convenience and comfort. The slots are 00–03, 03–06, 06–09, 09–12, 12–15, 15–18, 18–21 and 21–24 hours. Retain the fare/slot evidence required by the office. S8 (para 3)

Example: For a 09:00–12:00 slot, compare the eligible-class fares available at booking, prefer non-stop where appropriate, and document a choice within the applicable price-band provision. A 10% band is not permission to buy an otherwise ineligible class.

07 — Advance purchase and tour booking under 72 hours

Booking at least 21 days ahead is encouraged, not expressed as an absolute condition of entitlement. A tour booking made less than 72 hours before intended travel requires a self-declared justification. The order permits booking even while approval of the tour programme is under process; that does not eliminate the need for approval. S8 (paras 4–5)

Example: A ticket booked 48 hours before an official tour needs the stated justification; it is not automatically invalid solely because it was not booked 21 days ahead.

08 — Cancellation under 24 hours

Unnecessary cancellation should be avoided. A cancellation made less than 24 hours before intended travel requires a self-declared justification. This is a different clock from the under-72-hour booking justification. S8 (para 6)

Example: A ticket cancelled 12 hours before departure calls for the cancellation justification even if it was originally booked more than 21 days ahead.

09 — One ticket per leg and exceptional alternatives

The order prefers one ticket per leg and disallows holding more than one, with an exception for special exigencies or exceptional circumstances: at most two alternative flights in different time slots for the same leg, with self-declared justification. S8 (para 7)

Example: For a genuinely exceptional uncertain meeting time, two alternative-slot tickets may be considered only with the specified justification; three alternatives are outside this exception.

10 — Self-booking and official email

Tickets can be arranged by the office through an authorised agent. Employees are encouraged to use the self-booking tool or online booking portal of one of the three agents and must register their official Government email ID with those agents for digital booking. S8 (para 8)

Example: A self-booking officer uses an authorised portal registered with the official email, rather than assuming any commercial comparison site is an authorised booking agent.

11 — Unauthorised booking and competent relaxation

In unavoidable circumstances involving an unauthorised agent/website, the Financial Adviser of the Ministry/Department and the Head of Department not below Joint Secretary rank in subordinate/attached offices have the stated relaxation authority. Actual facts and the competent order matter; the rule does not promise approval or require automatic total forfeiture in every case. S8 (para 10)

Example: After an unavoidable booking through an unauthorised site, submit the justification and seek relaxation from the authority specified for the office; do not declare the claim automatically paid or automatically lost.

12 — Journey certificate, bill and settlement

The prescribed journey-completion certificate/undertaking is due within 72 hours of completion; the TA bill may follow under its own applicable filing rules. Ministries/Departments should settle the agent’s air-ticket dues within a maximum of 30 days after completion of the journey. The prescribed certificate warns that a false declaration can attract disciplinary action. S8 (paras 11–12 and Annexure 1)

Example: An officer returning Monday submits the completion certificate within 72 hours, while completing the TA bill through the separate office process.

13 — Mileage points and superseded directions

The 2017 TA annexure discussed use of mileage points earned on Government-funded tickets for Government purposes. The later 2022 air-booking OM says no mileage points will be generated against Government-account travel and supersedes existing instructions on its subject. Old Air-India-only language must not be treated as a current blanket airline rule after the 2022 private-airline clarification. S1 S8

Example: A 2026 Government-account booking is not a basis to claim private frequent-flyer rewards under the superseded older practice; check any subsequent instructions.

C. Foreign-visit approvals, exceptions and sponsorship

Approval rules are wider than TA rates and can change. The following is a paragraph-level teaching map of the located 5 January 2016 instructions, their 25 January 2016 clarification, and 2017/2019 timeline instructions—not an assurance that all later relaxations or ministry-specific powers have been found. S9 S11

14 — Plan and minimise the visit: 2016 paras 1–4

The 2016 instructions call for foreign-visit data and a quarterly rolling plan in FVMS, only essential visits, minimal delegation strength and duration, and consideration of available Indian Missions or video-conferencing. Appropriate functional-level officers should be selected. Check present administrative workflow and any updated portal. S9

Example: Before sending a five-member team, the Ministry checks whether the Mission abroad or a video meeting can accomplish part of the objective.

15 — Five days, five members and the training clarification

The 2016 para 5 states that a delegation exceeding five working days or five members is to be placed before the Screening Committee of Secretaries (SCoS). The 25 January 2016 clarification says SCoS approval is not required for officers up to Joint Secretary level as part of the foreign training component, mid-career training programme or other training, irrespective of number and days. Check the precise case and later instructions; this is not a waiver of every other approval. S9

Example: Do not apply the older 2014 “more than five trainees” instruction alone to a qualifying up-to-Joint-Secretary training component; check the later clarification and competent authority.

16 — Number of visits and international events: paras 6–8

The 2016 instructions set a norm of no more than four official foreign visits per officer in a year, with stated approval channels for exceeding it, and discourage participation in fairs/workshops/conferences unless essential. They address use of the host Mission and exceptional mobilisation of personnel. The January clarification uses the calendar year for counting. S9

Example: Before a proposed fifth official visit in the same calendar year, the office checks the correct SCoS/Prime Minister route and applicable exceptions, rather than assuming every fifth visit is impossible.

17 — Senior officers and SAARC/Myanmar: paras 9–15

The 2016 instructions address when Secretaries should travel, Parliament sessions and simultaneous absence of Minister and Secretary. They specify SCoS routing for Secretary/Additional Secretary proposals with stated SAARC/Myanmar exceptions, and special treatment if a Secretary accompanies a Minister. Composite Secretary/Additional-Secretary-led delegations must not be split to evade the prescribed review. FA/Minister approval is required as stated for cases referred to SCoS. S9

Example: If a Secretary accompanies the Minister on a qualifying SAARC visit, the office checks the specified exception and SCoS route rather than assuming all SAARC travel is exempt.

18 — PSU/autonomous bodies and non-officials: paras 16–18

The 2016 order exempts PSU/autonomous-body visits from SCoS procedure unless part of a composite delegation from the administrative Ministry. It also addresses who bears Ministry/Department officers’ foreign-visit expenses and requires Prime Minister approval for Government-funded non-official visits, with routing depending on whether the non-official is part of a composite delegation. These are scoped rules, not universal exemptions for all bodies. S9

Example: An autonomous-body officer joining a composite Ministry delegation must be checked under that composite-delegation provision, not treated automatically as independently exempt.

19 — International sponsors, foreign hospitality and personal invitations: paras 19–21

For the specified international bodies of which India is a member, bilateral/multilateral agreements or regular exchange programmes, para 19 permits acceptance of international air travel cost and hospitality; the terms on deputation are not to be supplemented with Government of India deputation terms as stated, and cash/local travel/hotel allowances follow the sponsor terms. The order treats certain direct invitations involving no Government business as personal visits, with leave and the specified senior-officer approval. Relevant political and FCRA clearances, if required, accompany proposals. Do not double-claim an expense paid by a sponsor. S9

Example: If a qualifying international body pays a ticket, the officer does not claim the same ticket a second time; the office separately examines the sponsor’s terms, visit sanction and hospitality clearances.

20 — Submission timeline and approval delegation: paras 22–25; 2019

Where the specified SCoS/Prime Minister approval is required, the 2019 OM directs proposals to be received 15 days before departure, but not later than 5 days before departure; proposals outside that timeframe are liable to rejection. The 2016 order also describes delegated decisions for Director/Joint-Secretary-level visits, states the spirit of the guidelines applies to specified bodies, and requires the delegation leader’s tour report and copies as stated. Verify current delegations and portal. S9 S12

Example: A qualifying proposal arriving two days before departure is outside the stated five-day outer limit and may be rejected; approval cannot be assumed because air tickets were booked.

D. MEA daily allowance for official foreign tours

21 — Start with the correct order and posting

The 2023 MEA order supersedes its stated 11 November 1996 order and says it is effective 21 September 2023. Its country rates are stated to be the same throughout a country. India-posted and overseas-posted tours use different duration schedules. Before a live claim, verify the order and country rate currently applicable, including departmental endorsement. S2

Example: A Delhi-based officer’s approved trip to Singapore needs the current MEA foreign schedule; the domestic ₹ food-table ceiling is not the Singapore foreign DA.

22 — Country rate: selected 2023 examples

The 2023 annexure lists country rates in US-dollar terms; the order also permits local-currency payment as described below. 23 — Pay-level rate and unresolved Levels 2–9

The 2023 order’s extracted para 3 gives Level 10 and above the full annexure rate; the extracted Level 1 line reads 33%. The scan extraction reads 75% for above Level 1 but below Level 10, but unofficial transcriptions report 79%. The conflicting middle row is unresolved until a legible authoritative copy is visually verified. Level 13A is equivalent to Level 13 for TA/DA under the DoE clarification. S2 S18

Example: Level 12 may begin a simple 2023 country-rate illustration with the full base, subject to other conditions. For Level 7, stop: request the authenticated rate and do not compute either 75% or 79% as fact.

24 — India-posted tour duration

For an officer posted in India, on a tour from India to one or more countries abroad, the 2023 order gives full admissible DA for the first 14 days; 75% for the next 14 days; 60% thereafter, for each tour. S2 (para 2(ii))

Example: If the relevant full admissible daily rate is assumed to be $100 and day 15 is a complete qualifying day, the unadjusted day-15 slab is $75. Do not infer partial-day counting, exchange rate or overlapping deductions from this simplified example.

25 — Overseas-posted tour duration

For the stated tours by an officer posted abroad, within the country of posting or from one Mission to another, the 2023 order gives full admissible DA for the first 7 days; 75% for the next 7; 50% thereafter. The India-posted 14-day schedule is different. S2 (para 2(i))

Example: At an assumed full daily rate of $100, complete qualifying day 8 is $75 and day 15 is $50 before other applicable conditions. Do not automatically restart the duration on a border crossing.

26 — Conveyance is separate, not unlimited

MEA para 4 says foreign DA does not include the cost of official-journey transport. Actual taxi/conveyance cost for duty trips may be reimbursed if the controlling authority considers it necessary and reasonable, subject to admissibility and specific provision of funds in the deputation/delegation sanction. S2

Example: A necessary taxi from the approved hotel to an official meeting may be separately considered when the sanction contains the funds; a sightseeing taxi does not become an official expense merely because the officer is abroad.

27 — Approved hotel panel and actual charges

The 2023 order prescribes hotel panels in major cities instead of a general monetary hotel-rental ceiling. It requires accommodation in an approved-panel hotel and reimbursement of actual room rental, including service charges, taxes etc. Check the current Mission panel, grade and records. S2 (para 5)

Example: An eligible officer stays in a hotel on the current Mission panel and submits the actual room invoice; a domestic ₹ hotel ceiling cannot simply be substituted for this panel rule.

28 — City without a panel; own or free accommodation

For a city without an approved panel, the order uses the lowest hotel rate for the officer’s level in the country’s capital city as the ceiling for a hotel in that city. Where the officer makes their own arrangement, or accommodation alone is provided free, DA is granted at the rate prescribed for the grade. Confirm current panel details and other qualifying conditions. S2 (para 5)

Example: For a no-panel city, the officer asks the Mission for the relevant level’s lowest capital-city hotel rate; free lodging alone does not by itself invoke the State-Guest/all-meals-free rule.

29 — Non-representational training or seminar

The hotel entitlement for non-representational visits such as training courses or seminars is one step below the normal entitlement. This is a hotel entitlement rule, not an assertion that every foreign DA percentage is reduced by one step. S2 (para 6)

Example: For an approved non-representational seminar, the Mission checks the one-step-lower hotel category for the officer’s normal grade.

30 — State Guest plus all meals free

When an officer is treated as a State Guest and provided all meals free, the 2023 order says only 25% of the DA rates is admissible. Both stated facts matter. S2 (para 7)

Example: If the officer is a State Guest and all meals are free, a simple $100 otherwise applicable rate gives $25 before checking any other applicable conditions. Free accommodation alone is a different situation under para 5.

31 — Tips and breakfast

There is no reimbursement of tips in addition to DA under the 2023 order. Where hotel charges include breakfast, DA is reduced by 10%. The order does not, in the quoted paragraphs, settle every possible overlap or sequencing question. S2 (paras 8–9)

Example: For a $100 applicable daily rate, if only the breakfast reduction applies, $90 remains. If State-Guest and breakfast provisions appear to overlap, obtain the department’s authoritative calculation instead of inventing a sequence.

32 — Currency and official exchange rates

Where the local currency is freely convertible, the US-dollar-equivalent DA may be paid in local currency, with the local equivalent arrived at via rupees using MEA’s official exchange rates fixed from time to time. At missions with non-freely-convertible local currencies, prescribed DA may be paid in US dollars. S2 (para 10)

Example: The accounts office uses the relevant official MEA exchange rate rather than an arbitrary airport-counter rate to convert an eligible dollar amount into freely convertible local currency.

33 — Concurrence, effective date and adoption

The 2023 order records DoE and MEA internal-finance concurrence and its 21 September 2023 effective date. The CSIR forwarding letter says CSIR’s implementation begins from its own endorsement. The fact that an indexed copy is hosted by CSIR does not by itself establish a universal CSIR adoption date for every civilian department. S2 (paras 11–12 and forwarding letter)

Example: An officer in another department confirms that department’s applicable order and date; they do not copy a CSIR-specific implementation date automatically.

Example: For a qualifying sanctioned scientific survey on a Government boat, the office checks the special food-rate-equivalent DA and approval separately from the ship's passenger-class entitlement.

F. Road: prescribed rates, fallback, route and adjacent rules

37 — Places with specific prescribed rates: Level 14+

The 2017 table gives Level 14+ actual fare by any type of public bus including AC, or the prescribed AC-taxi rate when the journey is actually by AC taxi, or the prescribed auto-rickshaw rate for the listed modes including auto, own car, scooter, motorcycle and moped. Routes, sanction and other applicable rules remain relevant. S1 (para 2(D)(i))

Example: A Level 14 employee actually using an AC taxi at a place with a prescribed rate checks that AC-taxi rate; an unused AC-taxi mode is not claimable merely because it is entitled.

38 — Levels 6–13 and 13A

The prescribed-rate table gives Levels 6–13 the same entitlement as the preceding row except AC-taxi journeys are not permissible under that row. Level 13A follows Level 13 for TA/DA under the separate clarification. Public AC bus and the listed auto-rate alternatives are not the same as an AC taxi. S1

Example: A Level 9 employee can check the public AC-bus option at a prescribed-rate place but cannot assume this row permits an AC taxi.

39 — Levels 4–5

The prescribed-rate table allows actual fare by any public bus other than AC, or prescribed auto-rickshaw rates for the listed modes, including auto, own car, scooter, motorcycle and moped. This is not the same as the Level 14 AC-taxi option. S1

Example: A Level 5 officer using a non-AC public bus checks the actual fare and applicable route cap; an AC bus is not covered by this particular row.

40 — Level 3 and below

The prescribed-rate row allows actual fare by ordinary public bus only, or prescribed auto-rickshaw rates for journeys by auto, own scooter, motorcycle, moped etc. Unlike the preceding rows, own car is not expressly listed in this row. Do not invent an own-car entitlement from another level’s list. S1

Example: A Level 3 employee on an ordinary public bus checks the actual eligible fare; a claim for own-car mileage requires a separate applicable authority.

41 — No specific State or neighbouring-State rate: 2017 fallback

Where no specific rate is prescribed by the Directorate of Transport of the concerned State or the neighbouring States, the 2017 fallback is ₹24/km for own car/taxi and ₹12/km for auto-rickshaw/own scooter etc. Its clause provides a 25% increase whenever DA increases by 50%. Eligibility, actual journey, other rules and the correct local rate still need checking. S1 (para 2(D)(ii))

Example: If an applicable prescribed State or neighbouring-State rate exists, start with that rate rather than automatically substituting the fallback.

42 — First 25% indexed fallback calculation

Dearness Allowance reached the 50% threshold from 1 January 2024; 60% from 1 January 2026 is not itself another 50-percentage-point trigger. For the 2017 fallback clause, one 25% increase calculates ₹24 × 1.25 = ₹30/km and ₹12 × 1.25 = ₹15/km, subject to applicability and later instructions. The Department of Posts implementation is a department-specific confirmation, not a universal consolidation for every service. S1 S14

Example: On a qualifying fallback journey by an eligible auto for 20 km, the formula gives 20 × ₹15 = ₹300 before any other applicable limits and proof checks. The formula does not authorise a mode not otherwise allowed.

43 — Separate foot mileage

The 2017 city-travel reimbursement provision says an allowance of ₹12/km on foot is payable additionally for journeys on foot and rises by 25% when DA rises by 50%. One such increase calculates ₹15/km, subject to its context, applicability and later instructions; it is not a general automatic payment for all walking. S1 (para 2(E)(iii)) S13

Example: An eligible official foot journey of 4 km, to which this provision applies, calculates 4 × ₹15 = ₹60, subject to sanction and evidence.

44 — Domestic city travel is not inter-city fallback

Within-city travel under domestic tour DA has its own mode and level-specific ceilings or mileage conditions; it is distinct from an inter-city journey under the road-mileage table. Level 9–11 city-travel self-certification is addressed by the 2020 DoE clarification. Foreign duty conveyance is a third, separate rule. S1 S2

Example: A Level 9 taxi within the tour city is checked under the domestic city-travel rules, not automatically as an unlimited ₹30/km inter-city journey; a foreign duty taxi is considered under MEA para 4.

45 — Transfer effects by road are not the employee's fare

For eligible transfer of personal effects, the 2017 base road rates are ₹50/km for Level 6 and above, ₹25/km for Level 5, and ₹15/km for Level 4 and below. One 25% increase calculates ₹62.50, ₹31.25 and ₹18.75 per km, respectively. These rates and conditions concern effects, not the employee’s separate road-travel fare or the Composite Transfer Grant. S1 (para 3(iii)) S13

Example: Level 7 eligible effects over 50 km calculate a ₹3,125 ceiling (50 × ₹62.50); actual qualifying transport, evidence, weight and other conditions must be checked before reimbursement.

46 — Effects evidence and the specified-region exception

The 2017 order requires receipts/vouchers for effects, including its named regions. The 16 June 2022 DoE clarification creates a specific one-third entitlement without mandatory receipts for qualifying cases of an employee transferring to/from the North Eastern Region, A&N, Lakshadweep or Ladakh without family; accompanied claims require receipts under that order. It is effective from its issue date, and settled past cases are not reopened. Check the exact applicability and later amendments. It is not a blanket waiver for every regional transfer. S1

Example: An eligible unaccompanied regional transfer may invoke the one-third no-receipt provision; an accompanied transfer cannot assume that exception applies merely because the destination is in the region.

47 — Rates, purpose and proof are separate

The road tables do not turn a personal excursion into official travel, or prove that a ride took place. The controlling/accounts authority applies the sanctioned purpose, permitted mode, actual route, fare/mileage and evidence requirements. A prescribed kilometre rate is not always an automatic reimbursement of the full amount. S1

Example: A 30 km private weekend trip during an official tour is not automatically eligible for the official road rate.

48 — Places not connected by rail

For places not connected by rail, the 2017 domestic travel conditions allow AC bus for those entitled to AC II Tier and above by train, and Deluxe/ordinary bus for others. Consider any individual saving or later instructions. S1 (para 2(A)(iii)(a))

Example: An officer whose eligible rail entitlement is AC II checks the AC-bus provision for a no-rail destination; a different rail entitlement must be assessed under the other row.

49 — Road public transport between rail-connected places

Between places connected by rail, the 2017 condition allows travel by any means of public transport provided total fare does not exceed the fare by the entitled train class. The clause is not worded as an automatic private-vehicle mileage cap. S1 (para 2(A)(iii)(b))

Example: A qualifying public-bus fare of ₹400 against an entitled-class train fare of ₹500 does not exceed this specific cap. Other mode, sanction and evidence rules still apply.

50 — Do not extrapolate the public-transport cap

The explicit public-transport train-fare condition in unit 49 should not automatically be applied to every private car or scooter mileage claim. Nor does its wording prove every private-vehicle claim has no other restriction. Seek the correct authority for the actual mode and route. S1

Example: For an approved own-car journey on a rail-connected route, the office checks the specific private-vehicle and mileage instructions instead of asserting either unlimited payment or the public-bus cap without authority.

G. Worked integrated cases and FAQs

51 — Foreign tour: duration and separate taxi

Rule: A Level 12 India-posted officer uses the applicable MEA country base and 14/14/thereafter schedule; necessary, reasonable duty conveyance is separate if specific sanction funds and admissibility requirements exist. S2

Example: Assume a $100 full qualifying country rate, no other adjustment and a complete day 15: the slab gives $75. A necessary official-meeting taxi is examined separately; neither the $75 nor the taxi is automatically payable without the actual conditions.

52 — Two different sea cases

Rule: The SCI mainland–island ship table and conventional other-place steamer table are not interchangeable. S1

Example: Level 7 on a qualifying SCI mainland–island ship checks First/A cabin. Level 5 on a conventional three-class steamer checks middle/second class.

53 — One route, three kinds of amount

Rule: The employee's inter-city fare, eligible domestic city travel and qualifying transfer-effects transport are different components with different tables and proof. S1 S16

Example: An officer’s public bus to a new station, a local official taxi and a truck carrying effects must be tested under their respective applicable provisions; do not add three generic mileage rates to the same expense.

54 — Can 2023 foreign country rates be called final 2026 rates?

Answer: Not from this research alone. The 2023 order is the latest relevant MEA order located, but later orders and department-specific adoption remain to be verified. S2

Example: Before sanctioning a 2026 Nepal tour, obtain the currently applicable MEA rate and department instruction, even though the 2023 annexure reads $100.

55 — Does a 60% Dearness Allowance mean another 25% hike?

Answer: No automatic second hike follows merely because Dearness Allowance rose from 50% to 60%. The relevant individual rate clause and its future 50-point trigger must be checked. Foreign daily allowance is separately regulated by MEA. S1

Example: The 2017 road fallback’s first indexed amounts calculate to ₹30/₹15 per km after one 25% increase; do not multiply them by 1.25 again just because DA is 60%.

56 — Do LTC or training orders automatically replace tour rules?

Answer: No. An approved tour, training, transfer, retirement and leave travel can have different conditions; the DoPT LTC framework is not a substitute for MEA foreign-tour DA. This batch identifies the official foreign-training hotel/approval provisions located, but does not claim to reproduce every training or LTC instruction. S1 S9

Example: An employee attending a sanctioned foreign seminar checks its non-representational hotel rule and sponsor terms; a personal holiday to the same city is not the same claim.

57 — What records and deadline should I check?

Rule: Keep the sanction, funding and necessary clearances, itinerary, relevant official fare/agent documentation, tickets and declarations, applicable hotel and conveyance evidence, and any self-certification. The general tour/training/transfer claim period is 60 days, with retirement generally 180 days, subject to relevant dates and exceptions; the 72-hour air-completion certificate is separate. S8 S19

Example: After returning from an air tour, an officer submits the completion certificate within 72 hours and separately checks the claim deadline and supporting documents with the office.
