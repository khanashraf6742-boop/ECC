Important dates first

  • 11.02.1958: OM 43/5/57: own-Controlling-Officer declaration procedure; 2025 consolidation also cites this date under Rule 5. Issue date, not a new 2025 benefit.
  • 24.06.1958: OM 43/15/57: normal-residence test, sequential criteria, multi-property and near-relations conditions.
  • 31.10.1958 / 1958-59: EXPIRED redeclaration deadline / historical entry block. Not a current deadline or a fresh-declaration loophole today.
  • 18.05.2015: OM 31011/4/2007 as cited in official 2025 FAQs 19-20: HQ=hometown and other-state NCR clarification.
  • 01.07.2025 / 03.09.2025: FAQ / consolidation issue dates, not blanket replacement or new commencement dates for every earlier rule.
  • Before journey starts: Rule 6 advance destination declaration; ordinary change requires CO approval. No universal 21/30-day lead time is created by Rule 6.
  • After journey starts: Rule 6 normally bars destination change; the express beyond-control/inability-to-request-earlier test and competent relaxation must be established.
  • 2026-2029: Current regular four-calendar-year block on 27.10.2026; sub-blocks 2026-27 and 2028-29. Fresh-recruit personal blocks differ.
  • 31.12.2026: Conditional first-year carryover deadline for eligible unused prior-block LTC. HT-entitled employees must satisfy Rule 10's applicable second-sub-block condition for All-India carryover. Detailed block accounting is Batch 5.

Scope, currentness and corrections

Scope: uploaded master chapter 9, Rules 5-6. Every authored card is spoken as exact reference, simple city-Hinglish explanation, then an immediate hypothetical example. Rule 5 and Rule 6 are unnumbered paragraphs in the full rules; descriptive teaching limbs are not invented statutory subrules.

Reviewed 27 October 2026 using the official government-hosted full rules/GIDs, the 3 September 2025 consolidation and official 1 July 2025 FAQ. Targeted searches did not identify a verified later amendment to the hometown/destination provisions; this is not a guarantee that no later instrument exists. Original 1958 Gazette/standalone OM scans are not claimed authenticated.

Material Rule 6 discrepancy: the 2025 summary combines before-commencement wording with an inability to request before commencement, an internally inconsistent sentence. The full government-hosted Rule 6 distinguishes (a) pre-start CO-approved changes, (b) the post-start prohibition and (c) the beyond-control inability-to-request-earlier exception, relaxable by Administrative Ministry/Department or HoD. The consolidation itself gives precedence to governing OMs/orders in paragraph 2. This edition discloses the discrepancy and uses the complete rule text; it does not pretend to have authenticated a new amendment correcting the summary. The sanctioning office should verify the governing provision in a disputed case.

Master corrections: its property-first mnemonic is not the legal criterion sequence. The sequence is obligations/visits, residential/joint-family property, near relations, then pre-service residence, moving on only if the preceding criterion is not satisfied. Its Ministry-only shorthand for Rule 6 relaxation omits HoD. Its absolute suggestion that no authority can ever allow a second change is not a full analysis of broader Rule 18 powers: Batch 3 teaches the ordinary Rule 5 one-change ceiling, not an automatic general relaxation.

The 31 October 1958 fresh-declaration concession and 1958-59 entry block are historical, expired transition provisions. They are explicitly excluded from current instructions, not revived as a way around the lifetime ordinary hometown-change limit.

Initial hometown acceptance, hometown-change authorisation, destination-change approval and advance-money sanction are different questions. A person may hold more than one designation, but must exercise the legally competent role. The CAG/Secretaries instruction concerns the expressly named communication route; it is not a blanket change-limit waiver for all own-CO officers.

FAQ 20 is not a licence to replace an accepted hometown with a current Noida/Gurugram residence without declaration/acceptance. FAQ 19 denies hometown LTC when HQ and hometown coincide; it does not by itself bar a valid available All-India entitlement. Special Dispensation conversion, current scheme validity and complete fresh-recruit/block mechanics are later-batch topics, not certified here.

The 2025 sources use then-current 2022-25 examples. As at this review, regular block 2026-29 and sub-blocks 2026-27/2028-29 apply; previous-block carryover is conditional, not a fresh entitlement or universal extension of every used block. Rule 6 gives an advance-declaration requirement, not a universal 21-day/30-day numeric deadline; follow separately applicable booking and office instructions.

All cases, sample dates and office worksheets here are invented teaching aids, not actual sanctioned claims or prescribed official forms. Relevant governing rules/orders prevail over summary wording; educational review is not a legal certainty guarantee or departmental sanction. Other topic GIDs remain pending batchwise.

B3-01

Hometown is not just a postal address

Rule 4(e); Rule 5: declaration and acceptance

Rule / current restatement: Hometown is the town, village or other place declared by the employee AND accepted by the Controlling Officer. An address, birthplace, Aadhaar entry or property purchase alone does not perform the required LTC declaration and acceptance.

Simple city Hinglish: Hometown employee declare kare aur Controlling Officer accept kare. Sirf postal address ya birthplace enough nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Ravi ka new flat Pune mein hai; flat purchase se accepted hometown Jaipur automatically Pune nahi hota.

Watch out: LTC hometown, residential address and birthplace are not interchangeable.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-02

Once accepted, the hometown is final

Rule 5: finality of accepted hometown

Rule / current restatement: The declared hometown, once accepted by the Controlling Officer, is treated as final. A later change must use the Rule 5 exception and competent authority; a fresh form cannot quietly replace the accepted record.

Simple city Hinglish: Accepted hometown final hai; next LTC form par naya naam likhkar record replace nahi kar sakte.

Immediate hypothetical example: Jaipur accepted hai; next form par Goa likhne se Goa hometown nahi banega.

Watch out: Finality is subject to the specific Rule 5 change mechanism, not personal preference.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-03

An unused change is not a free coupon

Rule 5: exceptional circumstances

Rule / current restatement: Rule 5 permits authorisation of a change in exceptional circumstances. Having never changed hometown is not, by itself, an entitlement to change it on demand. The authority must consider the circumstances and hometown test.

Simple city Hinglish: Change sirf exceptional circumstances mein authorise ho sakta hai; unused option automatic right nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Employee ne kabhi change nahi liya, par bas holiday preference badli hai; change guaranteed nahi.

Watch out: Exceptional circumstances and competent authorisation are separate from the numerical limit.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-04

Normal hometown-change authority: HoD

Rule 5: Head of Department authority

Rule / current restatement: For an employee who is not himself/herself Head of Department, the Head of Department may authorise the exceptional hometown change. Initial acceptance by the Controlling Officer is distinct from this change authority.

Simple city Hinglish: Normal employee ka hometown change Head of Department authorise karta hai; initial acceptance wala role alag hai.

Immediate hypothetical example: Section officer ne approval sign kiya; pehle check karo kya woh competent HoD capacity mein authorised hai.

Watch out: Head of Office, Controlling Officer and Head of Department are not automatically identical roles.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-05

If the employee is HoD: Administrative Ministry

Rule 5: employee is Head of Department

Rule / current restatement: If the government servant is himself/herself Head of Department, the Administrative Ministry is the authorising authority under Rule 5. The HoD cannot use the ordinary HoD provision to self-authorise that change.

Simple city Hinglish: Employee khud HoD hai toh hometown change Administrative Ministry authorise karegi; self approval nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Director khud HoD hai; apna Jaipur se Udaipur change apne signature se clear nahi karega.

Watch out: This is hometown-change authority, not merely where a copy of the declaration is sent.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-06

At most one change during service

Rule 5: once during service ceiling

Rule / current restatement: An authorised change under the ordinary Rule 5 power cannot be made more than once during the government servant's service. A transfer/new LTC block does not itself reset this allowance. Do not convert this into an unsupported assertion that every broader statutory relaxation power is impossible; Rule 18 is a separate later topic.

Simple city Hinglish: Normal Rule five power mein entire service ke dauraan maximum one change; transfer ya new block se reset nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Employee ek authorised change le chuka hai; next posting par second change normal Rule five se nahi milega.

Watch out: Maximum one is a ceiling, not an automatic right; broader relaxation is not decided here.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-07

Where would you normally live but for service?

Rule 5 GID (1), opening test; OM 43/15/57-Ests.(A), 24.06.1958

Rule / current restatement: The controlling test is whether the declared place is where the employee would normally reside but for absence due to government service. It is not simply the most convenient tourist place, current posting or place of birth.

Simple city Hinglish: Main test: Government service ki wajah se door na hote toh normally kahan rehte?

Immediate hypothetical example: Neha Delhi posting par hai, par normal home connection Kota ka hai; sirf Delhi job location decisive nahi.

Watch out: The test is reaffirmed in the 2025 consolidation; 1958 is not obsolete merely because old.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-08

Owning a house is not compulsory in every case

Rule 5 GID (1), paragraph 1: illustrative, not exhaustive

Rule / current restatement: Property ownership and permanent residence of relatives in the older 1956 instruction were illustrative, not exhaustive. Apply the normal-residence test and the ordered criteria; do not turn every clue into an additional compulsory condition.

Simple city Hinglish: Property aur relatives ke clues illustrative hain; har employee ke paas own house hona compulsory nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Neha ke paas Kota mein house nahi; sirf isi reason se baaki hometown evidence ignore mat karo.

Watch out: No four-out-of-four scorecard or universal compulsory property condition.

Evidence: H

B3-09

First: obligations and visits after joining

Rule 5 GID (1), criterion (i)

Rule / current restatement: Check whether the place requires physical presence at intervals for domestic/social obligations AND whether the employee has visited frequently since entering service. This is the first criterion, before property.

Simple city Hinglish: First criterion: domestic ya social obligations ke liye presence chahiye, aur service join karne ke baad frequent visits hue hon.

Immediate hypothetical example: Ravi Jaipur regularly family responsibilities ke liye jaata hai; yeh first criterion ka evidence ho sakta hai.

Watch out: Both the obligations and post-entry visiting pattern matter.

Evidence: H

B3-10

Second: own or joint-family residential property

Rule 5 GID (1), criterion (ii)

Rule / current restatement: If the preceding criterion is not satisfied, consider residential property owned by the employee or held by the joint family of which the employee is a member. This does not require individual ownership alone.

Simple city Hinglish: Next criterion residential property hai: own house ya employee ki joint family ka house.

Immediate hypothetical example: Individual title Ravi ke naam nahi, par woh residential property wali joint family ka member hai; yeh route check karo.

Watch out: Residential property here; the separate multiple-property paragraph also mentions landed property.

Evidence: H

B3-11

Third: near relations who live there

Rule 5 GID (1), criterion (iii)

Rule / current restatement: If preceding criteria are not satisfied, consider near relations resident at the place. Where this is the determining criterion, residence should be more or less permanent, not a brief visit.

Simple city Hinglish: Third criterion near relations ka residence hai; deciding basis ho toh fairly permanent connection chahiye.

Immediate hypothetical example: Kota mein near relations genuinely settle hain; short holiday visit se yeh fact alag hai.

Watch out: The source does not provide a universal invented list of documents or a fixed number of years here.

Evidence: H

B3-12

Fourth: living there before government service

Rule 5 GID (1), criterion (iv)

Rule / current restatement: If the earlier criteria are not satisfied, consider whether the employee lived at the place for some years before entering government service. A hospital birthplace or a brief stop is not the same factual question.

Simple city Hinglish: Fourth criterion: Government service se pehle employee wahan kuch years raha tha kya?

Immediate hypothetical example: Neha Kota mein several years rahi thi before service; sirf hospital mein birth hona same fact nahi.

Watch out: Read in the ordered test, not as a mandatory extra condition in every case.

Evidence: H

B3-13

Correct sequence: visits, property, relations, residence

Rule 5 GID (1), NOTE: sequential application

Rule / current restatement: The criteria are applied one after another only where the immediately preceding criterion is not satisfied: (i) obligations/visits, (ii) residential/joint-family property, (iii) near relations, (iv) pre-service residence. The master's property-first memory hook is not the legal sequence.

Simple city Hinglish: Order visits, property, near relations, pre service residence hai. Previous criterion satisfy na ho tab next par jao.

Immediate hypothetical example: First criterion satisfy hai; phir bhi chaaron ko compulsory bana kar rejection mat karo.

Watch out: The uploaded master's property-first mnemonic conflicts with its own correctly ordered text.

Evidence: H

B3-14

Several properties: give reasons; CO decides

Rule 5 GID (1), multiple-property paragraph

Rule / current restatement: Where the employee or the family of which he/she is a member owns residential or landed property in more than one place, the employee may choose a place with reasons. The Controlling Officer's decision to accept or reject that choice as hometown is final.

Simple city Hinglish: Employee ya jis family ka woh member hai, unki residential ya landed property multiple places par ho, toh reasons ke saath choice do; CO accept ya reject karega.

Immediate hypothetical example: Jaipur aur Kota dono jagah family property hai; employee Kota choose karke reasons de, automatic acceptance nahi.

Watch out: Choosing a place is not the same as authorising a later change to an already accepted hometown.

Evidence: H

B3-15

A visiting relative is not permanent residence

Rule 5 GID (1), near-relations permanence paragraph

Rule / current restatement: When near relations are the determining criterion, their presence should be of a more or less permanent nature. This is not satisfied merely by temporarily arranging for relatives to visit a preferred destination.

Simple city Hinglish: Near relations ko deciding basis bana rahe ho toh unka stay fairly permanent hona chahiye, temporary holiday nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Aunt ek week Shimla ghoomne aayi hai; us visit se Shimla ka permanent family connection establish nahi hota.

Watch out: No invented universal six-month, one-year or domicile-certificate threshold.

Evidence: H

B3-16

31 October 1958 is NOT a current deadline

Rule 5 GID (1), paragraphs 2-3: expired historical transition

Rule / current restatement: The one-time redeclaration window ended on 31.10.1958; approved declarations within that transition were treated as first declarations, not changes, and the newly eligible concession started from block 1958-59. These expired transition provisions are explained only to prevent misuse, not offered as present benefits.

Simple city Hinglish: Thirty one October nineteen fifty eight redeclaration window expired hai; nineteen fifty eight to fifty nine block historical hai.

Immediate hypothetical example: Aaj new declaration ko old transition bolkar one change limit bypass nahi kar sakte.

Watch out: Excluded from current instruction in the GID register; old does not mean every paragraph is still usable.

Evidence: H

B3-17

Own Controlling Officer: go to the next superior

Rule 7 GID (4), first sentence; OM 43/5/57-Ests.(A), 11.02.1958

Rule / current restatement: An officer who is his/her own Controlling Officer for TA purposes sends the initial or subsequent hometown declaration to the next superior administrative authority for acceptance. Being own CO is not a general self-acceptance licence.

Simple city Hinglish: Officer apna own Controlling Officer ho toh initial ya subsequent hometown declaration next superior administrative authority ko acceptance ke liye bheje.

Immediate hypothetical example: Officer TA mein own CO hai; hometown acceptance ke liye sirf apne aap sign karke file close nahi kare.

Watch out: For an actual hometown change, Rule 5 authorisation also remains relevant.

Evidence: H

B3-18

Direct submission to Accounts is not the normal acceptance route

Rule 7 GID (4), already-sent declarations sentence

Rule / current restatement: The same superior-authority procedure also applies to own-CO officers who already sent declarations directly to Accounts Officers. Mere filing with Accounts does not substitute for the prescribed acceptance process; examine and regularise the record through the proper authority.

Simple city Hinglish: Own CO officer ne pehle declaration direct Accounts ko bheji thi, tab bhi prescribed superior acceptance procedure follow karna hai.

Immediate hypothetical example: Accounts mein declaration ki copy mil gayi; sirf receipt ko competent acceptance ka substitute mat maano.

Watch out: Do not automatically cancel past sanctioned claims; inspect actual acceptance and the applicable procedure.

Evidence: H

B3-19

CAG and Secretaries: special communication route

Rule 7 GID (4), CAG and Secretaries exception sentence

Rule / current restatement: The Comptroller and Auditor General and Secretaries to Government communicate their initial hometown declaration and subsequent declarations for change directly to the Accounts Officers concerned. This is the expressly named communication exception; it is not a blanket exemption for every own-CO officer or a stated waiver of Rule 5's change limit.

Simple city Hinglish: Comptroller and Auditor General aur Secretaries to Government initial aur subsequent change declarations direct concerned Accounts Officer ko communicate karte hain.

Immediate hypothetical example: Regional Director own CO hai, par Secretary to Government nahi; direct Accounts wali special exception assume nahi kare.

Watch out: Communication route is distinct from the applicable legal change-authorisation requirement.

Evidence: H

B3-20

All-India destination: declare before travel

Rule 6: scope, self or any family member

Rule / current restatement: When LTC to any place in India is proposed by the employee or any family member, the employee must declare the intended place of visit in advance to the Controlling Officer. Family-only travel is not exempt.

Simple city Hinglish: All India LTC self ya family ke liye ho, intended place employee advance mein Controlling Officer ko declare kare.

Immediate hypothetical example: Sirf wife Kochi jaa rahi hai; employee travel nahi kar raha, phir bhi destination declaration chahiye.

Watch out: A fixed accepted hometown is not a substitute for declaring an All-India destination.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-21

Destination approval is not advance-money sanction

Rule 6: recipient and advance timing; Rule 4 GID (2)(i) distinction

Rule / current restatement: Rule 6 addresses the Controlling Officer and requires advance declaration; it specifies no universal 21-day or 30-day lead time. Separate booking advice, office procedures and Head-of-Office LTC advance-money authority must not be substituted for this declaration.

Simple city Hinglish: Destination Controlling Officer ko advance declare karo. Rule six khud universal twenty one ya thirty day deadline nahi deta.

Immediate hypothetical example: Head of Office ne money advance sanction kiya; isse destination declaration automatically complete mat samjho.

Watch out: Follow separately applicable administrative/booking requirements; no invented Rule 6 day count.

Evidence: H

B3-22

Before departure: CO approval for destination change

Rule 6: change before commencement

Rule / current restatement: Before commencement, the declared place may be changed with the Controlling Officer's approval. The ordinary pre-commencement limb does not require importing the stringent post-commencement exception. Approval, not merely notification, is required for the change.

Simple city Hinglish: Journey start se pehle declared destination Controlling Officer ki approval se badal sakte hain.

Immediate hypothetical example: Goa declared tha; departure se pehle Kochi change chahiye, toh CO approval lo, sirf ticket edit nahi.

Watch out: This is an All-India destination change, not a service-record hometown change.

Evidence: H

B3-23

After travel starts: no ordinary destination switch

Rule 6: prohibition after commencement

Rule / current restatement: After commencement, the declared destination may not ordinarily be changed. A route/flight change and a change of declared place are different questions; a new ticket or later claim entry is not itself permission.

Simple city Hinglish: Journey start ke baad destination normally change nahi; ticket change karna legal approval ka substitute nahi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Goa ke liye journey start hui; holiday preference se Kochi jaakar claim mein quietly naam change mat karo.

Watch out: Assess the express exception separately; do not teach an absolute no-exception rule.

Evidence: H

B3-24

Exceptional circumstances: why could you not request earlier?

Rule 6: post-commencement exception conditions

Rule / current restatement: The exception requires exceptional circumstances establishing that the change request could not be made before commencement owing to circumstances beyond the employee's control. A change of mind or merely late paperwork is not enough. Facts and competent relaxation must be assessed.

Simple city Hinglish: Exception mein establish karo ki beyond control circumstances se change request journey se pehle nahi ho sakti thi.

Immediate hypothetical example: Journey start ke baad sudden closure hua; dated evidence ke saath exceptional relaxation seek karo, approval automatic nahi.

Watch out: A genuine disruption is evidence for consideration, not a guaranteed sanction.

Evidence: H

B3-25

After-start exception: Ministry/Department or HoD

Rule 6: authority for post-commencement relaxation

Rule / current restatement: The Administrative Ministry/Department or the Head of Department, as the case may be, may grant this relaxation. Do not rely on ordinary CO approval alone; if one person holds both roles, the competent capacity in which approval is given matters.

Simple city Hinglish: After start exception Administrative Ministry, Department ya Head of Department relax kar sakte hain; sirf normal CO approval par rely mat karo.

Immediate hypothetical example: CO ne okay likha, par competent HoD capacity mein sanction nahi; proper authority check karo.

Watch out: HoD remains a named option; the master's short Ministry-only shorthand is incomplete.

Evidence: H

B3-26

Known preference before departure is not beyond control

Rule 6: inability to request before departure — contrast case

Rule / current restatement: The timing condition asks why a request could not be made before travel. A preference already known before commencement, with no beyond-control inability to request, does not establish that exception merely because the form was filed later.

Simple city Hinglish: Pehle se known holiday preference par request late di, toh beyond control exception automatically nahi banta.

Immediate hypothetical example: Employee departure se week pehle Goa ki jagah Kochi chahta tha, par request nahi di; baad mein exception assume mat karo.

Watch out: Office must evaluate the actual facts; there is no invented universal automatic waiver.

Evidence: H

B3-27

Once-in-service limit is NOT a destination-change rule

Rules 5 and 6: hometown versus destination-change limits

Rule / current restatement: Rule 5's once-during-service hometown-change ceiling does not become a lifetime cap on All-India destinations. Rule 6 governs intended destinations and changes for the relevant journey; each occasion must otherwise be available.

Simple city Hinglish: Hometown change ka once in service cap, All India destinations par lifetime cap nahi hai.

Immediate hypothetical example: Available occasion par is block Goa aur later valid block Kochi choose karna second hometown change nahi.

Watch out: Different destination does not create an extra LTC occasion or ignore Rule 6.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-28

Different family destinations still need declaration

Rule 6 read with Rule 11: separately declared family destinations

Rule / current restatement: Employee and eligible family members may visit different places under the applicable All-India entitlement; the place for each intended claim should be declared by the employee in advance. Separate travel does not remove Rule 6 or supply extra entitlement.

Simple city Hinglish: Family different destinations choose kar sakti hai, par intended claims ke places advance declare karo; extra occasion nahi banta.

Immediate hypothetical example: Employee Goa, eligible spouse Kochi jaaye; declaration mein dono ke actual intended destinations clear rakho.

Watch out: Full blocks and separate-travel accounting follow in Batch 5.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-29

HQ equals hometown: no hometown LTC

FAQ 19; OM 31011/4/2007-Estt.(A-IV), 18.05.2015

Rule / current restatement: Employees whose headquarters/place of posting and hometown are the same are not eligible for hometown LTC. Do not infer that all All-India LTC is also barred; its independent entitlement and availability must be checked under the scheme.

Simple city Hinglish: HQ aur accepted hometown same ho toh hometown LTC nahi. Isse available All India LTC automatically barred nahi hoti.

Immediate hypothetical example: HQ Delhi, accepted hometown Delhi: local Delhi visit ko hometown LTC mat label karo.

Watch out: Do not manufacture HT eligibility merely to convert it under a special scheme; scheme detail is deferred.

Evidence: F25

B3-30

Other-state NCR towns are not Delhi HQ

FAQ 20; OM 31011/4/2007-Estt.(A-IV), 18.05.2015

Rule / current restatement: Cities/towns outside Delhi and in other NCR states are not to be treated as Delhi headquarters for this purpose. The FAQ recognises hometown-concession eligibility in that situation. A mere current residential address still does not silently replace the accepted hometown under Rules 4(e)/5.

Simple city Hinglish: Delhi ke bahar other states ke NCR towns ko Delhi HQ mat treat karo; accepted hometown ka record separately check karo.

Immediate hypothetical example: HQ Delhi aur accepted hometown Noida, Uttar Pradesh hai; sirf NCR label se same HQ kehkar reject mat karo.

Watch out: Not every current NCR residence is automatically an accepted hometown; normal eligibility still applies.

Evidence: F25; H

B3-31

Flag the compressed 2025 timing sentence

Rule 6 full text; 03.09.2025 consolidation paragraph 2

Rule / current restatement: The 2025 Rule 6 summary says the place may be changed before commencement in circumstances where the request could not be made before commencement, an internally inconsistent compression. The full government-hosted Rule 6 separately states ordinary pre-start CO approval and exceptional post-start Ministry/Department/HoD relaxation. Paragraph 2 says governing OMs/orders prevail over imprecision. No new signed amendment reversing the full rule was authenticated in this review.

Simple city Hinglish: Twenty twenty five summary ki timing line inconsistent hai. Full Rule six ka before aur after start distinction padho; governing order prevail karta hai.

Immediate hypothetical example: Summary ki compressed line se CO approval ko after start ka blanket waiver mat banao; office se applicable rule verify karo.

Watch out: Discrepancy is disclosed, not silently edited or presented as a newly authenticated amendment.

Evidence: H; G25

B3-32

One case, three distinct approval questions

Rules 4(e), 5 and 6: worked office checklist, not a new subrule

Rule / current restatement: Keep accepted hometown, any authorised service-record change and the All-India journey destination in separate fields. Check competent authority and chronology for each. This checklist is teaching guidance, not a new numbered statutory subrule or a new universal documentary schedule.

Simple city Hinglish: Accepted hometown, authorised hometown change aur All India destination ko alag fields mein rakho; authority aur dates match karo.

Immediate hypothetical example: Jaipur hometown hai, HoD ne Udaipur change authorise kiya; All India Goa se Kochi pre start change ke liye CO approval alag chahiye.

Watch out: Record actual approvals and circumstances; the PDF sample is not an official prescribed form.

Evidence: H; G25
