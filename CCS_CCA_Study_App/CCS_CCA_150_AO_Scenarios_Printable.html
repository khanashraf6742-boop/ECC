150 applied scenarios for the Administrative Officer's desk

Companion workbook to CCS (CCA) + DoPT Suspension · 1,300 MCQs · Applied AO Edition · Questions Q1151–1300 · 15 linked cases of ten decisions · printable, no scripts

How to work this workbook. Read the case facts once, then answer the ten decisions without looking ahead. Write the letter you choose in the margin. Use the answer section at the end to mark your work; read the explanation only for decisions you got wrong or guessed. A realistic pace is one case in eight minutes.

Three-step audit for every order you write at work: (1) Competence — does the signatory hold the power? (2) Procedure — which rule route applies, and which safeguard is triggered now? (3) Reasons — what must appear on the face of the order so that it can be defended? Most of the distractors in this set are one of those three steps skipped.

Case 61 · Appointing authority and the competence file

Case factsAn Administrative Officer must certify competence before any disciplinary action proceeds. In these files the post, the appointing authority and the order-maker are stated. Assume the CCS (CCA) Rules apply, no Schedule entry or Presidential delegation displaces the default position unless stated, and the facts are admitted.
Decision 1Rule 2(a)

A post has two authorities competent to make appointments to it; the officer was in fact appointed by the junior of the two. For disciplinary purposes the file must identify the appointing authority as:

  1. the highest of the authorities competent to make appointments to that service or post
  2. the authority that presently controls the officer's attendance
  3. whichever authority the officer names in his representation
  4. the junior authority that actually signed the appointment order
Decision 2Rule 12(4)(a)

The appointing authority's subordinate proposes to impose removal on an officer of the same service. The proposal records that the appointing authority was kept informed. The order is:

  1. valid, because the appointing authority was merely informed of the proposal
  2. without jurisdiction; such a penalty needs the appointing authority
  3. valid, if the subordinate authority is named as disciplinary authority in the Schedule
  4. valid, provided the officer was heard on the penalty proposed
Decision 3Rule 13(2)

A Deputy Secretary competent only to impose minor penalties signs the order instituting proceedings and appointing an inquiry officer in a case that may end in dismissal. The institution is:

  1. void unless the appointing authority later ratifies the charge-sheet
  2. void, because only the appointing authority may institute a major-penalty case
  3. competent under Rule 13(2), because a minor-penalty authority may institute proceedings for major penalties
  4. competent only after a Review Committee clears the file
Decision 4Rule 12(3)(a)

A Group 'C' employee, other than a member of the Central Secretariat Clerical Service, works in a Ministry. No Schedule entry or Presidential order names a special authority. Who may impose a Rule 11 penalty?

  1. only the appointing authority, with no alternative route
  2. only the immediate head of the section where he works
  3. only the President, because Group 'C' penalties require higher sanction
  4. the Secretary to the Government of India in that Ministry, among the authorities Rule 12(3) names
Decision 5Rule 12, Explanation II

An officer holding a Group 'C' post is promoted, on an officiating basis, to a Group 'B' post. A minor-penalty question arises. Explanation II to Rule 12 requires the file to treat him as:

  1. belonging to the higher Group for the purposes of that rule
  2. outside Rule 12 until the probation period ends
  3. belonging to both Groups, requiring both authorities' concurrence
  4. remaining in the lower Group until confirmation
Decision 6Rule 12(4)(b)

An officer substantively appointed to a General Central Service post is temporarily appointed to another Service. The authority in the temporary Service proposes a major penalty without consulting anyone. The defect is:

  1. failure to obtain the President's prior approval
  2. failure to consult the authority competent but for the temporary appointment
  3. no defect, because a temporary appointment transfers full competence
  4. failure to consult the lending State Government before the penalty
Decision 7Rule 12(3)(b)

The head of an office is lower in rank than the authority competent under Rule 12(2). A censure order is signed by that head of office. The order is:

  1. competent, because censure is the lightest penalty
  2. competent, because the head of office is the immediate controlling authority for discipline
  3. not competent: Rule 12(3)(b) excludes a head of office junior to the Rule 12(2) authority
  4. valid if the employee consents to that forum in writing
Decision 8Rule 13(1)

A Ministry wants a subordinate authority to institute proceedings against an officer of a different cadre. There is no Presidential general or special order of empowerment. The lawful route is:

  1. institution by the Central Vigilance Commission
  2. institution by the officer's immediate superior in every case
  3. a Ministry-level note authorising any subordinate authority to institute
  4. institution by an authority competent under the rules, or a direction traceable to Rule 13(1)
Decision 9Rule 12(1); Rule 22(i)

The President personally imposes censure on a Group 'A' officer. The officer asks whether the penalty itself is incompetent. The correct advice is:

  1. the penalty is within Rule 12(1); what Rule 22(i) bars is an appeal against a Presidential order
  2. the penalty is void because censure always requires a Rule 14 inquiry
  3. the penalty is void unless a committee of secretaries concurs
  4. the penalty is void because the President may impose only major penalties
Decision 10Rule 14(21)(a)-(b)

An inquiry held by an authority competent only to impose minor penalties finds that a major penalty is warranted. The AO must send the record to:

  1. the appellate authority, for a decision on quantum
  2. the disciplinary authority competent to impose the major penalty, which may act on the evidence or recall witnesses
  3. the President in every such case, because a major penalty always needs the highest sanction
  4. the Review Committee, for a recommendation on guilt

Case 62 · Suspension file: orders, review and pay

Case factsA suspension file arrives with the order, a review diary and a subsistence claim. Assume Rule 10 and FR 53 govern, all dates are admitted, and no charge-sheet under the CCS (CCA) Rules has been issued unless the file says so.
Decision 1Rule 10(1); Rule 12, GOI decision (1)

An officer holding only current charge of a post, not substantively appointed to it, signs a suspension order. The validity question turns on:

  1. whether the Review Committee had already met and recommended continuation
  2. whether the officer to be suspended was heard before the order was signed
  3. whether the signatory held the statutory power at all, since current duty confers none
  4. whether the appointing authority later approved the order in writing
Decision 2Rule 10(5)(c)

A subordinate authority revokes a suspension ordered by its own superior. The revocation is:

  1. effective, because the disciplinary authority ratified the revocation later
  2. effective, because the Review Committee had recommended revocation
  3. effective, because a revocation always operates in the employee's favour
  4. ineffective, because only a higher authority in that line may revoke it
Decision 3Rule 10(5)(b)

Suspension is continuing for one proceeding when a second disciplinary proceeding begins. To keep the officer under suspension until both end, the file needs:

  1. a direction by the competent authority for reasons recorded in writing
  2. a fresh suspension order by the inquiry officer
  3. a Review Committee resolution alone
  4. an automatic extension worked by the second charge-sheet
Decision 4Rule 10(2)(b)

An officer is convicted and sentenced to two years' imprisonment; the department has not dismissed him. His status from the date of conviction is:

  1. ordinary duty until a formal suspension order is signed by the appointing authority
  2. deemed suspension from the date of conviction, because no terminal penalty followed
  3. automatic dismissal from the date of conviction by the trial court
  4. deemed suspension from the date of release from prison
Decision 5Rule 10(3)

A terminal penalty imposed on a suspended officer is set aside in appeal and the case is remitted for further inquiry. The officer claims he was never under suspension during the interval. Correct advice:

  1. suspension restarts only from the date of the appellate order of remission
  2. no suspension can exist once the original penalty has been set aside
  3. Rule 10(3) deems the suspension to continue from the date of the original order
  4. suspension continues only if a fresh suspension order is issued
Decision 6Rule 10(4), proviso

A court sets aside removal on purely technical grounds without examining merits. The disciplinary authority wants a further inquiry. Under Rule 10(4) and its proviso:

  1. permissible without any assessment of the court's reasoning on the file
  2. permissible, but only with the leave of the court that set aside the removal order
  3. barred, because any judicial order setting aside the penalty forecloses further inquiry
  4. permissible only for a technical-ground decision, with suspension deemed from the original order
Decision 7FR 53(1)(ii)(a)(i)

After three months of suspension the competent authority finds the delay is not attributable to the officer. The subsistence component of Rs 20,000 may be increased by:

  1. up to 50 per cent of the allowance admissible during the first three months, on a reasoned assessment
  2. up to 25 per cent of that component, applying the same cap used for a reduction
  3. nothing at all, because the first-three-month figure is frozen for the whole suspension
  4. up to 100 per cent automatically once three months of suspension are completed
Decision 8FR 53(2)

The suspended officer does not furnish the prescribed certificate about other employment for four months, and payroll continues full subsistence. The audit objection is:

  1. no certificate is needed while a criminal case against him is pending
  2. payment depends on the certificate, so the claim cannot be paid as a matter of course
  3. the certificate concerns his income-tax position, not subsistence allowance
  4. the certificate becomes necessary only after one year of suspension
Decision 9Rule 10, GOI decision (2)

A suspended officer shifts to his home town without permission, arguing that suspension is not a posting. The AO's position is:

  1. he may reside anywhere provided he reports weekly to the office
  2. a suspended officer has no headquarters at all while the order runs
  3. he must stay at the headquarters fixed for him, normally his last place of duty
  4. his headquarters shifts to the place of his choice on suspension
Decision 10Reinstatement and regularisation instructions; FR 54-B

A suspension order is revoked on 5 June. The officer claims full pay for the entire suspension period because revocation proves the order was wrong. The AO's correct approach is:

  1. the period must always be regularised as ordinary leave on half pay
  2. no pay at all is admissible for any part of a suspension period
  3. full pay follows automatically, because a revoked order must have failed
  4. pay and treatment follow the competent authority's findings, not the revocation itself

Case 63 · Drafting and instituting the charge-sheet

Case factsA charge memo is being drafted on the AO's desk. Each file states the imputations, the evidence and the stage reached. Assume a major penalty is proposed unless stated, and that no Rule 19 route is invoked.
Decision 1Rule 14(3)(i)-(ii)

A draft charge memo narrates the conclusion ('misappropriated funds') without stating facts, documents or witnesses. The defect is that Rule 14(3) requires:

  1. definite and distinct articles, with imputations listing documents and witnesses
  2. the inquiry officer's tentative findings on each allegation framed
  3. a statement of the penalty proposed, inside the charge memo itself
  4. a single consolidated article that covers all the allegations together
Decision 2Rule 14(3)(i)

Two unrelated incidents from different years are merged into one article of charge. The AO should:

  1. keep them merged, to save the inquiry time and paper
  2. split them into separate articles that can each be answered distinctly
  3. leave the framing of the articles entirely to the presenting officer
  4. drop the older incident without examining it on the record
Decision 3Rule 14(4); cited instructions

A charge memo requires a written statement of defence within three days and omits any reference to being heard in person. Under Rule 14(4) read with the cited instructions, the AO should:

  1. omit the personal-hearing question, since the defence is in writing
  2. keep the three days, because the rule itself fixes no period
  3. require a reasonable period, normally fifteen days, and ask about being heard in person
  4. require the written defence to be filed only at the first hearing
Decision 4Rule 14(6)

An inquiry officer starts the inquiry without receiving the written statement of defence, the witness statements or the order appointing the presenting officer. Which duty was skipped?

  1. the Review Committee's duty to brief the inquiry officer on the file
  2. the appellate authority's duty to transmit the original record
  3. the employee's duty to serve copies on the presenting officer directly
  4. the disciplinary authority's duty under Rule 14(6) to forward the listed papers
Decision 5Rule 14(7)

The inquiry officer fixes the first appearance eleven working days after receipt of the reference and refuses any extension. The rule position is:

  1. appearance within ten working days, extendable by not more than ten days
  2. no extension of the first appearance is ever permissible at all
  3. appearance may be fixed within thirty days in every case
  4. any period the inquiry officer prefers is lawful on the facts
Decision 6Rule 16(1)(a)

A censure proposal rests on a note that the officer 'has been careless'. No written intimation of the proposal or the imputations was given, though the file records that his explanation was 'not called for'. Under Rule 16(1)(a):

  1. the procedure is complete, because censure is too light a penalty to need notice
  2. the procedure is defective for want of written intimation and a representation opportunity
  3. the procedure is governed only by Rule 14, so Rule 16 never applies to censure
  4. the procedure is complete, because the officer had signed the file earlier
Decision 7Rule 16(1-A)

The competent authority proposes to withhold increments for five years. The AO must advise that:

  1. no inquiry is needed for any withholding of increments at all
  2. the Rule 16 written procedure alone is sufficient in this case
  3. a Rule 14 inquiry is mandatory, because the withholding exceeds three years
  4. only withholding with cumulative effect needs a regular inquiry
Decision 8Proviso to Rule 14(2); Rule 3C, CCS (Conduct) Rules

A complaint of sexual harassment under the Conduct Rules is received, and the AO appoints a deputy secretary as inquiry officer. Under the proviso to Rule 14(2):

  1. the case must be handled only under the Rule 19 route
  2. the complaint must first be referred to the police in every case
  3. any officer of deputy secretary rank may be appointed to inquire
  4. the Complaints Committee is deemed the inquiring authority in such a case
Decision 9Rule 14(1)

A removal order records that the officer was 'heard at length by the Secretary', but no inquiry was held and Rule 19 is not invoked. The order is:

  1. unsustainable, because a major penalty needs an inquiry under Rule 14 and 15
  2. sustainable only if the Secretary happens to be the appointing authority
  3. sustainable, because removal is not among the Rule 11 penalties at all
  4. sustainable, because a personal hearing is an adequate substitute for an inquiry
Decision 10Rule 14(5)(a)

The officer admits every article of charge in his written statement, and the authority imposes a penalty the same day without recording findings. The defect is:

  1. no findings are needed once every charge has been admitted in the defence
  2. findings on each charge must still be recorded before a penalty is imposed
  3. the admission converts the case into a Rule 19 proceeding at once
  4. an inquiry officer must still be appointed in every such admitted case

Case 64 · Defence assistance and inquiry logistics

Case factsAn inquiry officer's file reaches the AO with requests for assistance, documents and witness statements. Assume a regular Rule 14 inquiry, that the presenting officer is not a legal practitioner unless stated, and that every date is recorded in the order sheet.
Decision 1Rule 14(8)(a)

The charges involve complex accounts and the officer asks for a lawyer. The presenting officer is not a legal practitioner and the disciplinary authority has not permitted legal assistance. The request must be:

  1. allowed only where the appellate authority grants its prior approval
  2. allowed automatically wherever the charges are of a serious or complex nature
  3. declined as of right, because a lawyer is permitted only in the stated situations
  4. allowed automatically if the officer says he cannot afford a lawyer
Decision 2Proviso to Rule 14(8)(a)

The officer wants a colleague posted in another city to assist him. The colleague is willing. The inquiry officer refuses without giving reasons. The refusal is:

  1. invalid, because permission is automatic once the colleague consents
  2. valid, because the officer may engage only a retired Government servant
  3. valid, because a defence assistant must always be from the headquarters office
  4. defective in form, because an out-station permission needs recorded reasons
Decision 3Note to Rule 14(8)(a)

The chosen defence assistant is already assisting in three pending disciplinary cases. Under the Note to Rule 14(8)(a), the officer:

  1. may not take that Government servant's assistance
  2. may take it because the limit applies only to legal practitioners
  3. may take it if the three cases belong to another department
  4. may take it with the inquiry officer's permission
Decision 4Rule 14(8)(b)

The officer proposes to be assisted by a retired Government servant. The inquiry officer rejects the request outright as impermissible. The correct position is:

  1. it is never permissible because assistance must come from serving staff
  2. it is permissible, subject to conditions specified by the President by general or special order
  3. it requires the disciplinary authority to conduct the inquiry itself
  4. it is permissible without any conditions
Decision 5Rule 14(11)

After service of the charge the officer neither appears nor pleads. The inquiry officer's correct next step is to:

  1. treat the non-appearance as an admission of every article of charge
  2. close the record and recommend a major penalty on the papers
  3. produce the evidence and adjourn the case for not more than thirty days
  4. refer the file to the disciplinary authority for a Rule 19 decision
Decision 6Note to Rule 14(11)(ii)

The officer asks orally for copies of the statements of the listed witnesses. Copies must be supplied:

  1. only at the appellate stage, along with the original record
  2. only if the inquiry officer decides that the request is reasonable and necessary
  3. only after the witnesses have already been examined
  4. as early as possible, and not later than three days before their examination
Decision 7Rule 14(11)(i)

The defense wants to inspect the documents in the list served with the charge. The inspection facility is:

  1. within five days of the order, extendable by not more than five more days
  2. within twenty-four hours of the first hearing in the inquiry
  3. only after the disciplinary authority's case has been closed
  4. at any time before the inquiry report is written
Decision 8Rule 14(11)(iii); Note thereunder

The defence wants a register that was not included in the list. The correct route is:

  1. request the presenting officer to add it to his own list of documents
  2. give notice within ten days, stating the relevance of the documents
  3. write directly to the custodian of the record, outside the inquiry
  4. make an oral request at any time during the course of the inquiry
Decision 9Proviso to Rule 14(12)

The inquiry officer refuses to requisition a document because he considers it irrelevant. To be sustainable, the refusal must:

  1. be reported to the disciplinary authority before issuing
  2. be approved by the presenting officer
  3. be supported by reasons recorded in writing
  4. be communicated only to the custodian
Decision 10Rule 14(13), proviso

A custodian of requisitioned records certifies that their production would be against public interest. Under Rule 14(13):

  1. the Government servant must approach the appellate authority for the record
  2. the record is placed on the file with the objection noted
  3. the inquiry officer may compel production after a written warning to the custodian
  4. communicate it to the officer and withdraw the requisition

Case 65 · Evidence, examination and the inquiry report

Case factsThe inquiry has closed and the record is being checked before the report goes to the disciplinary authority. Assume the articles of charge were properly framed and served, and that no Rule 19 route applies.
Decision 1Rule 14(14)

The presenting officer, in re-examination, asks questions on an entirely new matter without the inquiry officer's leave. The AO should record that:

  1. re-examination on a new matter requires the inquiry officer's leave
  2. the answers must be struck from the record in every case
  3. re-examination is permitted only on the defence's request
  4. re-examination is unrestricted once cross-examination occurs
Decision 2Note to Rule 14(15)

After the department's case closes, the presenting officer seeks to produce a register 'to complete the gaps' in the evidence. The request should be:

  1. granted, because the inquiry officer may call any evidence at any time
  2. refused, because new evidence may not be called to fill up a gap in the evidence
  3. refused only if the defence objects in writing
  4. granted if the register is a government record
Decision 3Rule 14(15)

New evidence is admitted in the inquiry officer's discretion. The Government servant becomes entitled to:

  1. an adjournment of thirty days in every case where new evidence is admitted
  2. a fresh charge-sheet and a new inquiry officer before the evidence is used
  3. a copy of the further-evidence list, an adjournment of three clear days, and inspection of the documents
  4. nothing at all, because further evidence is a departmental privilege
Decision 4Rule 14(18)

The officer declines to examine himself in his defence. After his case closes, the inquiry officer's duty is to:

  1. refer the file for a Rule 19 decision
  2. recall the departmental witnesses for further cross-examination
  3. record that no defence was offered and close the inquiry
  4. generally question him on the circumstances appearing against him in the evidence
Decision 5Rule 14(19)

Both sides wish to file written briefs instead of oral arguments. Under Rule 14(19) the inquiry officer:

  1. may hear them or permit written briefs if they so desire
  2. may permit briefs only with the disciplinary authority's sanction
  3. may permit briefs only for the presenting officer
  4. must hear them orally and refuse written briefs
Decision 6Rule 14(20)

The officer fails to submit a written statement and also fails to appear. The inquiry officer holds the inquiry in his absence. This course is:

  1. permitted only with the disciplinary authority's prior sanction
  2. permitted, as Rule 14(20) allows an ex parte inquiry on such default
  3. permitted only where Rule 19 has been invoked
  4. impermissible, because an inquiry requires the officer's presence
Decision 7Rule 14(22)

The original inquiry officer is transferred after recording most of the evidence. The successor wants to restart the whole inquiry. The correct position is:

  1. the inquiry lapses automatically on the transfer of the inquiring authority
  2. the successor must always re-record the entire body of evidence
  3. act on the recorded evidence, recalling witnesses only if justice requires
  4. the disciplinary authority must appoint a fresh presenting officer first
Decision 8Rule 14(23)(i)

An inquiry report records conclusions on each charge but omits the defence on each article and any assessment of evidence. The report is deficient because Rule 14(23)(i) requires:

  1. the disciplinary authority's tentative reasons for any disagreement
  2. a recommendation of the specific penalty to be imposed
  3. only the findings, with the evidence summarised by the presenting officer
  4. the charges, the defence on each charge, an assessment of the evidence, and findings with reasons
Decision 9Explanation to Rule 14(23)(i)

The evidence establishes a charge materially different from the framed article. The inquiry officer may record a finding on the different article only if:

  1. the underlying facts were admitted, or a reasonable opportunity to defend was given
  2. the presenting officer applies before the report is signed
  3. the new article carries a lesser penalty than the one originally framed
  4. the disciplinary authority approves the new article in advance
Decision 10Rule 14(24)

The inquiry has run for five months when the inquiry officer seeks an extension. Under Rule 14(24):

  1. no time limit applies once the evidence has begun to be recorded
  2. six months from receipt, extendable on recorded reasons by up to six months at a time
  3. the extension requires the Government servant's written consent to it
  4. the extension must not exceed ninety days in total for the whole inquiry

Case 66 · Minor-penalty desk: the Rule 16 checklist

Case factsA minor-penalty proposal is on the AO's desk. Each file states the penalty proposed and the procedure followed. Assume the proposed penalty is one of clauses (i) to (iv) of Rule 11 unless stated otherwise.
Decision 1Rule 16(1)(a)

A censure is imposed on the basis of a noting that the officer was 'casual in attitude'. No written intimation of the proposal or imputations was given. The order is:

  1. valid, because his confidential report already records the same conduct
  2. valid, because censure is so light that no procedure at all is required
  3. vitiated, because Rule 16(1)(a) requires written notice and a representation opportunity
  4. valid if he later signed the order merely acknowledging its receipt
Decision 2Rule 16(1); Rule 16(1-A)

The competent authority proposes withholding of one increment for two years, not affecting pension and without cumulative effect. The procedure required is:

  1. no procedure, because increments are not part of pay
  2. a Rule 14 inquiry only if the officer demands it
  3. a full Rule 14 inquiry with a presenting officer
  4. the Rule 16(1) written procedure, without a Rule 14 inquiry
Decision 3Rule 16(1-A)

The proposal is to withhold increments with cumulative effect for two years. The AO must record that:

  1. a Rule 14 inquiry is required before the order
  2. the penalty is not permissible at all
  3. an inquiry is required only if pension is affected
  4. the written procedure alone is sufficient because the period is short
Decision 4Rule 16(1-A)

The proposed withholding of increments is likely to affect the officer's pension. The file must show:

  1. that the officer was informed and given a representation opportunity only
  2. that an inquiry under sub-rules (3) to (23) of Rule 14 preceded the order
  3. that the penalty was limited to one year
  4. that the pension sanctioning authority concurred
Decision 5Rule 16(2)(vii)-(viii)

The minor-penalty record is being compiled. Which item is required by Rule 16(2)?

  1. the presenting officer's appointment order in every case
  2. the inquiry officer's personal notes and working papers
  3. the findings on each imputation, with reasons recorded in the order
  4. the appellate authority's tentative view
Decision 6Rule 16(1)(d)

The Commission's advice is received in a minor-penalty case. Before the order, the officer must be:

  1. given the advice and thirty days to respond to it
  2. given the advice only if he asks for it
  3. informed only when the penalty order is communicated
  4. supplied the advice, with fifteen days to respond in writing
Decision 7Rule 16(1)(b)

Although no inquiry is mandatory, the disciplinary authority considers one necessary in a minor-penalty case. It may:

  1. hold an inquiry in the manner laid down in sub-rules (3) to (23) of Rule 14
  2. not hold an inquiry, because Rule 16 excludes it
  3. hold an inquiry only if the penalty is later enhanced
  4. hold an inquiry only after obtaining the President's sanction
Decision 8Rule 15(5)

A full Rule 14 inquiry has been held and the authority decides on a minor penalty. Rule 15(5) provides that the authority:

  1. must restart the case afresh under Rule 16
  2. it may impose the minor penalty despite anything in Rule 16
  3. must obtain the officer's consent to the penalty
  4. must refer the file to the Commission yet again
Decision 9Explanation to Rule 11; Rule 23(v)(a)

An officer is stopped at the efficiency bar for unfitness to cross it. He claims a penalty was imposed without Rule 16 procedure. The correct answer is:

  1. the stoppage is a major penalty and requires a Rule 14 inquiry
  2. the stoppage is a minor penalty and requires a representation opportunity
  3. the stoppage is not a penalty under the Rule 11 Explanation, so Rule 16 procedure does not apply
  4. the stoppage is valid only if recorded as censure
Decision 10Rule 16(1)(e); Rule 16(2)(viii)

A minor-penalty order reads: 'Censure. He was careless.' No findings on the imputation are recorded. The order is:

  1. valid if the appellate authority later agrees with the penalty imposed
  2. valid if the file contains the officer's representation
  3. valid, because a censure order needs no reasons on its face
  4. unsustainable: findings on each imputation and reasons are required

Case 67 · One transaction, several officers, and the Rule 19 gate

Case factsAn AO handles a single transaction involving more than one officer, and separately screens files in which the department wishes to skip the ordinary inquiry. Assume the CCS (CCA) Rules apply and that no court has restrained the proceedings.
Decision 1Rule 18(1)

Two officers are concerned in one transaction and the authority is competent to dismiss both. A common proceeding may be ordered because:

  1. Rule 18(1) permits it where two or more Government servants are concerned in any case
  2. common proceedings require the President's sanction in every case
  3. common proceedings are available only for identical penalties
  4. common proceedings need the consent of both officers
Decision 2Note to Rule 18(1)

Two authorities are each competent to dismiss one of the officers involved. To make a common proceeding order:

  1. any one of them may do so unilaterally
  2. the highest of them may do so with the consent of the others
  3. the senior-most officer's disciplinary authority must act alone
  4. the President must always make the order
Decision 3Rule 18(2)

A common proceeding order names the disciplinary authority but says nothing else. Rule 18(2) additionally requires specification of:

  1. the appellate authority for each of the officers concerned
  2. the date by which the inquiry must be completed and reported
  3. the penalties it may impose and the procedure that will be followed
  4. the identity of the authority that will review the case
Decision 4Rule 18, GOI instruction (1)

Two officers have made cross-complaints against each other arising from the same incident. The cited instruction favours:

  1. a single inquiry with one officer as presenting officer
  2. dropping the later complaint
  3. one joint proceeding with both as accused
  4. separate proceedings tried simultaneously or in quick succession
Decision 5Rule 19(i), first proviso; Rule 19, GOI decision (1)

An officer has been convicted by a criminal court. The department proposes to impose a penalty without an inquiry under Rule 19(i). Before the order, the file should record that:

  1. an opportunity to represent, and the authority's own proportionality assessment
  2. only the appellate authority may consider whether the penalty is proportionate
  3. no representation is permissible at all once a criminal court has convicted him
  4. the conviction fixes the penalty, so proportionality is irrelevant here
Decision 6Rule 19(ii)

Witnesses have become unavailable and the disciplinary authority concludes that a regular inquiry is not reasonably practicable. Under Rule 19(ii) the conclusion must:

  1. be endorsed by the Central Vigilance Commission
  2. be recorded by the disciplinary authority with reasons in writing
  3. be approved by the inquiry officer before closure
  4. be taken by the President on the file
Decision 7Rule 19(iii)

The department wants to dispense with inquiry on the ground that holding it is inexpedient in the interest of the security of the State. Under Rule 19(iii):

  1. the appellate authority may record it
  2. the disciplinary authority may record it
  3. that satisfaction must be reached by the President
  4. the head of department may record it with reasons
Decision 8Rule 19, second proviso

A Rule 19 order is proposed and Commission consultation is necessary. Under the second proviso to Rule 19:

  1. the Commission itself decides the penalty in a Rule 19 case
  2. the advice may simply be enclosed with the final order instead
  3. consultation replaces the need for any representation by the officer
  4. consultation, plus an opportunity to represent against the advice
Decision 9Rule 19(ii); Rule 19, GOI decisions

Case 68 · From inquiry report to a sustainable final order

Case factsAn inquiry report has been received and the disciplinary authority's draft order is being vetted. Assume a regular Rule 14 inquiry, that the Commission is consulted where necessary, and that no appeal has yet been filed.
Decision 1Rule 15(2)

The inquiry officer exonerates the officer and the disciplinary authority proposes to disagree and impose a penalty. Before deciding, the authority must:

  1. obtain the Commission's concurrence in every case
  2. record its own findings and impose the penalty without further reference
  3. forward the report with tentative disagreement reasons, giving fifteen days
  4. remit the case to the inquiry officer for a fresh report
Decision 2Rule 15(2)

The report is wholly favourable, and the disciplinary authority accepts it and closes the case. The requirement of a fifteen-day representation opportunity:

  1. applies only where the Commission was consulted during the inquiry
  2. applies only if the officer requests a copy of the report
  3. does not apply at all, because no penalty is proposed
  4. still applies, because Rule 15(2) does not depend on the report being adverse
Decision 3Rule 15(1)

The disciplinary authority is dissatisfied with the inquiry and wants further evidence on one article. Under Rule 15(1) it may remit the case, but only:

  1. for reasons recorded by it in writing
  2. if the officer does not object
  3. after obtaining the Commission's approval
  4. with the inquiry officer's consent
Decision 4Rule 15(2); Rule 17

The disciplinary authority proposes removal and the file shows that the officer never received a copy of the report. The order is:

  1. valid, because the officer attended the inquiry and knows the evidence against him
  2. vitiated; the report and disagreement reasons must precede the decision
  3. valid, because the penalty is within the authority's competence
  4. valid, because Rule 17 communication after the order cures any such omission
Decision 5Rule 15(3)(b), (4)

The Commission's advice supports a major penalty. The disciplinary authority encloses the advice with the final penalty order. The AO should flag that:

  1. advice need not be disclosed where it supports the penalty
  2. enclosure with the order satisfies the requirement
  3. the advice had to be forwarded before the decision, with fifteen days to respond to it
  4. the advice is confidential and cannot be disclosed to the officer
Decision 6Rule 17

The final order records only 'removal from service' and conveys no findings on the articles or reasons for disagreement. The order is:

  1. valid, if the penalty is within the Schedule entry for the post
  2. valid, because the inquiry report already contains its findings
  3. valid, because reasons are needed only in appellate orders
  4. defective, because the findings and disagreement reasons were not supplied
Decision 7Rule 17

The disciplinary authority accepts the penalty advice but rejects part of the Commission's reasoning, and the communication says nothing about this. Under Rule 17 the communication should also contain:

  1. a brief statement of the reasons for not accepting the Commission's advice

  3. the Commission's internal noting
  4. a fresh charge-sheet on the rejected reasoning
Decision 8Rule 15(6)

After a full inquiry, the officer demands a separate hearing on the proposed major penalty before the order. Under Rule 15(6):

  1. a separate hearing is mandatory in every major-penalty case
  2. not required, because the findings and evidence already supply the basis

  4. a separate hearing is required only if the penalty is dismissal

The inquiry was instituted by a subordinate disciplinary authority under Rule 13(2), and a different officer of the same rank now proposes to pass the final order. The file should record that:

  1. the inquiry officer may pass the final order on his own report
  2. any officer of the same rank may pass the final order on the inquiry record

  4. the appellate authority must pass the final order in every case

Before signing a major-penalty order, the AO finds that the officer's representation on the report was received but never placed on the record. The order must be:

  1. signed with a note that the representation will be considered in appeal
  2. signed, because the inquiry report already addressed the points
  3. signed, because the representation was received after the fifteen-day period
  4. held back until the representation is considered and findings recorded

Case 70 · Deciding the appeal

Case factsAn appeal is pending before a competent appellate authority. Each file states what the record shows and what the authority proposes. Assume the appeal was filed in time and is in the prescribed form.
Decision 1Rule 27(2)(a)

The appellate authority in a penalty appeal finds that the procedure was followed but a procedural lapse caused prejudice to the defence. Under Rule 27(2)(a) it must consider:

  1. only whether the penalty is proportionate
  2. only whether the findings are supported by evidence
  3. procedural non-compliance and any resulting failure of justice
  4. whether the inquiry officer was of a higher rank than the officer charged
Decision 2Rule 27(2)(b)

The findings rest on no evidence at all. The appellate authority's Rule 27(2)(b) conclusion should be that:

  1. the case must be remitted for a fresh charge-sheet
  2. the findings must be accepted because the inquiry officer saw the witnesses
  3. the penalty is excessive but the findings stand
  4. the findings are not warranted by the evidence on record
Decision 3Rule 27(2)(c); Rule 27(2)(i)

The findings are sound, but the appellate authority considers removal disproportionate to a proved procedural lapse. Under Rule 27(2)(c) and (2)(i) it may:

  1. examine the adequacy, inadequacy or severity of the penalty and reduce it
  2. only set aside the penalty and exonerate
  3. only remit the case to the disciplinary authority
  4. only confirm the penalty, because findings stand
Decision 4Rule 27(2), proviso (ii)

The appellate authority proposes to enhance censure to removal, and no Rule 14 inquiry was ever held. Before the order it must:

  1. enhance directly, because the appellate power includes enhancement
  2. direct a Rule 14 inquiry first, subject to Rule 19 and the safeguards
  3. remit the case to the disciplinary authority without any inquiry
  4. obtain the disciplinary authority's consent and then enhance
Decision 5Rule 27(2), further proviso

A valid Rule 14 inquiry was held and the appellate authority now proposes a major-penalty enhancement. The additional safeguard is:

  1. the disciplinary authority's prior concurrence
  2. a fresh Rule 14 inquiry in every case
  3. a reasonable opportunity to represent against the proposed penalty
  4. a personal hearing at the appellate authority's discretion
Decision 6Rule 27(1)

In an appeal against suspension, the officer asks the appellate authority to decide the final punishment now. Under Rule 27(1) the authority:

  1. can only recommend revocation to the disciplinary authority
  2. must await the inquiry report before passing any order
  3. decides the final punishment because the appeal covers the whole case
  4. examine whether the suspension is justified, and confirm, modify or revoke it
Decision 7Rule 27(3)

An appeal against an order treating the suspension period as not counting as duty comes up. The order is not a Rule 11 penalty. The disposal standard is:

  1. it considers all the circumstances and passes just and equitable orders
  2. no appeal at all lies against such an order
  3. the appellate authority must remit the case in every instance
  4. the appellate authority may only confirm or set aside
Decision 8Rule 27, GOI instruction (2)

An appellant in a major-penalty appeal insists on an oral hearing. Under the cited instruction, the request is:

  1. an absolute right in every major-penalty appeal
  2. discretionary, to be considered on the specific request in the circumstances
  3. available only if the penalty exceeds five years
  4. impermissible, because appeals are decided on paper
Decision 9Rule 28

The appellate authority reduces the penalty and sends the order to the disciplinary authority, which takes no action for three months. Under Rule 28:

  1. implementation requires a fresh order by the disciplinary authority
  2. implementation is the duty of the appellate authority
  3. implementation is the duty of the authority that made the order appealed against
  4. implementation awaits the employee's application
Decision 10Rule 29(1)(v)

The appellate authority has disposed of the appeal, and a separate file on the same officer is later placed before it for revision under Rule 29(1)(v). The window for that revisional power is:

  1. within ninety days of the appellate order
  2. any time, because the reviser is the appellate authority
  3. within forty-five days of the order
  4. within six months of the date of the order proposed to be revised

Case 71 · Revision and review desk

Case factsFiles are marked 'revision' or 'review'. The AO must first identify the correct statutory route and the authority competent to use it. Assume the CCS (CCA) Rules apply and that any Commission consultation that is necessary has been identified separately.
Decision 1Rule 29(2)(i)

A reviser proposes to revise a penalty order two weeks after it was made, while the appeal period is still running and no appeal has been filed. Under Rule 29(2):

  1. revision may proceed with the employee's consent
  2. revision may proceed if the reviser is senior to the appellate authority
  3. revision cannot be commenced until the period of limitation for an appeal has expired or a preferred appeal is disposed of
  4. revision may proceed immediately because it is a superior power
Decision 2Rule 29(2)(ii)

A timely penalty appeal is pending before the appellate authority when a reviser proposes to reduce the penalty. The correct course is:

  1. reduce the penalty by parallel revision
  2. withdraw the appeal and then revise
  3. refer both files to the Commission for allocation
  4. await disposal of the appeal before commencing revision
Decision 3Rule 29(1); Rule 2(d)

A reviser wants to reduce a penalty. An office note says the Central Vigilance Commission's concurrence is always required for revision. The correct position is:

  1. there is no universal CVC-concurrence requirement; the check is statutory competence, the recording of reasons and any applicable Commission consultation
  2. CVC concurrence is mandatory in every revision case
  3. CVC concurrence replaces the need for Commission consultation
  4. revision is impossible without CVC concurrence
Decision 4First proviso to Rule 29(1)

A reviser proposes to enhance censure to withholding of increments. The officer has not been heard against the proposal. Under the first proviso to Rule 29(1):

  1. an opportunity is needed only for major penalties
  2. a reasonable opportunity to represent against the proposed penalty is required before the order
  3. no opportunity is needed because revision is an administrative power
  4. an opportunity is needed only if the officer requests it
Decision 5First proviso to Rule 29(1)

A reviser proposes a major penalty, but no Rule 14 inquiry has ever been held. Under the first proviso to Rule 29(1):

  1. the reviser may impose it after merely recording reasons
  2. the reviser must remit the case and take no further part
  3. no such penalty may be imposed except after a Rule 14 inquiry, subject to Rule 19, and after any necessary Commission consultation
  4. the reviser may impose it because revisional powers are wide
Decision 6Second proviso to Rule 29(1)

A Head of Department proposes revision of an order passed by an authority that is not subordinate to him. Under the second proviso to Rule 29(1) he:

  1. may revise any order in his department
  2. may revise if the employee consents
  3. may revise after obtaining the President's approval
  4. cannot exercise the power unless the authority which made the order in appeal, or the authority to which an appeal would lie, is subordinate to him
Decision 7Rule 29(3)

A revision application is filed by the employee. Under Rule 29(3), it must be dealt with:

  1. in the same manner as if it were an appeal under the rules
  2. as an administrative representation with no procedural rights
  3. only by the President
  4. as a fresh inquiry
Decision 8Rule 29-A

A retired officer petitions a departmental Head under Rule 29-A because he considers the penalty severe. The petition is:

  1. maintainable only after revision is exhausted
  2. misconceived as to forum and threshold, because Rule 29-A review is Presidential and requires qualifying new material that changes the nature of the case
  3. maintainable, because severity is a ground for review
  4. maintainable before any authority senior to the disciplinary authority
Decision 9Proviso to Rule 29-A

In a Rule 29-A review the President proposes to convert a minor penalty into a major one without any inquiry. The correct requirement is:

  1. only a representation against the proposed penalty
  2. remission of the case to the disciplinary authority
  3. a Rule 14 inquiry, subject to Rule 19, plus any necessary Commission consultation and an opportunity to respond to the advice
  4. no inquiry, because the President's power is plenary
Decision 10Rule 29(1)(v)

An appellate authority disposed of an appeal in January and now, in November of the same year, wants to revise the same order. Rule 29(1)(v) does not assist because:

  1. an appellate authority can never revise its own order
  2. revision requires the employee's application
  3. revision is barred for one year after an appeal
  4. the six-month revisional window runs from the date of the order proposed to be revised

Case 72 · When a penalty is set aside

Case factsOrders in these files have been set aside in appeal, revision or by a court, and the AO must now work out what follows on the disciplinary record. Assume the setting-aside order is final, that its own directions are as stated, and that the CCS (CCA) Rules apply.
Decision 1Rule 28

An appellate authority sets aside a dismissal and directs reinstatement, saying nothing about the intervening period. The first duty of the AO is to:

  1. implement the order first, then have the competent authority decide the period
  2. treat the whole period as duty because the dismissal has fallen
  3. treat the whole period as extraordinary leave without any order, since no duty was rendered
  4. await a fresh charge-sheet before any reinstatement at all
Decision 2Rule 29(1)(c); Rule 14

A revisional order sets aside a major penalty and directs a fresh inquiry from the stage of evidence. The earlier findings on the articles:

  1. stand until the officer objects to their being relied on
  2. fall with the order set aside, so the fresh inquiry must reach its own findings
  3. survive as conclusions that the fresh inquiry merely confirms after hearing him once again
  4. become conclusive once the reviser has read the whole record
Decision 3Proviso to Rule 10(4)

A court sets aside a removal on a technical ground and the department proposes to proceed afresh. Before any fresh inquiry, the file must establish:

  1. that the appellate authority has already cleared a fresh inquiry
  2. that the court, while setting the order aside, expressly permitted a fresh inquiry into the same articles
  3. that the judicial order went purely on technical grounds, not on the merits
  4. that the officer has consented in writing to a fresh inquiry
Decision 4Rule 11(vii); Explanation to Rule 11

Compulsory retirement imposed as a penalty under Rule 11 is set aside and the officer is reinstated. The AO must not treat the original order as:

  1. a penalty at all, because the officer has since been reinstated with all consequential benefits
  2. an order that required any communication to the officer
  3. an order that the officer could ever have appealed against
  4. retirement on superannuation: a penal compulsory retirement is a Rule 11 penalty
Decision 5Rule 28; Rule 23(ii)

An appeal substitutes censure for a removal already given effect. The AO must now:

  1. correct the service record and consequential entries to show censure
  2. leave the original entries standing, because they were valid when made
  3. record both penalties, leaving the officer to explain the difference
  4. await a fresh disciplinary order imposing censure in place of removal
Decision 6Rule 12(4)(a); Rule 13

A penalty is set aside solely because the authority that imposed it was not competent. The same authority now proposes to hold a fresh inquiry and impose the penalty. This is:

  1. permissible if the officer does not object to that forum
  2. impermissible: a fresh proceeding needs an authority competent to impose it
  3. permissible once the file has been endorsed by the appellate authority in writing
  4. permissible, because the earlier order has been set aside
Decision 7Rule 10(3)

A suspension was continuing when the penalty was set aside and the case remitted for further inquiry. The officer claims the suspension lapsed with the penalty. The position is that:

  1. the suspension continues only if a fresh suspension order is signed
  2. the suspension lapsed when the penalty was set aside
  3. it is deemed to continue from the original penalty until further orders
  4. the suspension revives only from the date of the fresh inquiry order
Decision 8Rule 28; promotion and sealed-cover instructions

After reinstatement on a set-aside, an officer claims the promotion he would have earned had the penalty not been passed. The AO's correct position is that:

  1. reinstatement automatically carries the promotion he would have earned but for the penalty
  2. no promotion can be considered until a fresh inquiry is concluded
  3. the appellate authority must sanction any promotion in the case
  4. reinstatement restores service; promotion is for the competent authority to decide
Decision 9Rule 29(1); confidential-report instructions

A revisional order sets aside a penalty on the very allegations reflected in adverse remarks in the officer's report. The AO should record that:

  1. the remarks survive; a separate representation on them must be decided
  2. the remarks stand until a fresh inquiry re-examines them
  3. the remarks are automatically expunged by the setting-aside of the penalty itself
  4. the remarks can be expunged only by the revising authority
Decision 10Rules 12–16

An appellate order criticises the inquiry officer's handling of the record but sets aside the penalty only on a procedural point. The AO proposes to start disciplinary action against the inquiry officer. The correct course is:

  1. ignore the criticism entirely, since an observation in an appellate order is only obiter dicta
  2. treat it as material; action against him needs its own charge and procedure
  3. refer the matter to the appellate authority for punishment
  4. treat the criticism as a finding and impose a penalty on him

A warning is recorded in an officer's report. The officer asks that it be treated as a penalty and set aside. The correct answer is:

  4. a warning is void because no inquiry preceded it

An order of censure must be communicated. Where the officer evades service at the office and at his residence, the AO should:

The copy of the order served on the officer carries no date of delivery. An appeal is later challenged as time-barred. The difficulty is that:

  4. the appeal period runs from the date the file was closed

An appellate record is called for and the office sends only the penalty order, keeping the inquiry papers back. The defect is that:

A file is being closed under Part IX provisions: notice service, a missed procedural time limit and an old saving question. Which provisions govern?

  1. Rule 30 for service, Rule 31 for relaxation of time, and Rules 33–34 for saving
  2. Rule 22 for exclusions from the service, Rule 27 for disposal of appeals and Rule 29 for revision

  4. Rule 14 for the inquiry and Rule 18 for common proceedings

A Rule 18 common proceeding ends with different penalties for different officers. The record must contain:

  3. the disciplinary authority's note, without separate findings for each
  4. a single consolidated finding covering every officer in the case

An officer asks for copies of the whole inquiry record after the final order. The AO's correct approach is to:

  1. refer the request to the appellate authority for orders on the file
  2. refuse every request once the case has been finally closed at the desk

  1. the penalty stands even if the record cannot be reconstructed at all, because the order is final

  3. a fresh inquiry is the only permissible course in every such case

A suspension is revoked and the period is regularised as duty on the footing that the suspension was wholly unjustified. Full pay follows because:

A suspension is revoked, the authority records that it was not wholly unjustified, and the officer is treated as on duty for the period. His pay for that period is regulated by:

An appellate order sets aside a penalty and directs that the period between dismissal and reinstatement be treated as duty. The AO must:

An order holds that a suspension period shall not count as duty for a specified purpose. The officer's remedy is:

  2. the Rule 23 clause on subsistence, apart from the suspension appeal

  4. Rule 23(i), because the matter concerns a suspension

An officer is reinstated after a set-aside and claims leave for the suspension period that he never applied for. The AO should record that:

A penalty of withholding of increments is set aside. The officer's increments were withheld for two years and his pay stagnated. The AO must:

Case 61 · Appointing authority and the competence file

Decision 1Rule 2(a)

A post has two authorities competent to make appointments to it; the officer was in fact appointed by the junior of the two. For disciplinary purposes the file must identify the appointing authority as:

  1. the highest of the authorities competent to make appointments to that service or post
  2. the authority that presently controls the officer's attendance
  3. whichever authority the officer names in his representation
  4. the junior authority that actually signed the appointment order

Answer A. the highest of the authorities competent to make appointments to that service or post

Rule 2(a) defines the appointing authority by competence to make appointments and, where several authorities are competent, selects the highest; the signatory is not decisive.

Trap. Actual appointment by a junior authority does not displace the Rule 2(a) highest-authority test.

Decision 2Rule 12(4)(a)

The appointing authority's subordinate proposes to impose removal on an officer of the same service. The proposal records that the appointing authority was kept informed. The order is:

  1. valid, because the appointing authority was merely informed of the proposal
  2. without jurisdiction; such a penalty needs the appointing authority
  3. valid, if the subordinate authority is named as disciplinary authority in the Schedule
  4. valid, provided the officer was heard on the penalty proposed

Answer B. without jurisdiction; such a penalty needs the appointing authority

Rule 12(4)(a) bars any authority subordinate to the appointing authority from imposing penalties in clauses (v) to (ix), apart from the Comptroller and Auditor-General exception for specified Indian Audit and Accounts Service cases.

Trap. Being informed is not the same as possessing the competence to impose; a Schedule entry does not cure subordination.

Decision 3Rule 13(2)

A Deputy Secretary competent only to impose minor penalties signs the order instituting proceedings and appointing an inquiry officer in a case that may end in dismissal. The institution is:

  1. void unless the appointing authority later ratifies the charge-sheet
  2. void, because only the appointing authority may institute a major-penalty case
  3. competent under Rule 13(2), because a minor-penalty authority may institute proceedings for major penalties
  4. competent only after a Review Committee clears the file

Answer C. competent under Rule 13(2), because a minor-penalty authority may institute proceedings for major penalties

Rule 13(2) expressly allows an authority competent to impose minor penalties to institute proceedings for major penalties, even though it cannot impose them.

Trap. Institution and imposition are separate competences; the record is later routed to the authority that can punish.

Decision 4Rule 12(3)(a)

A Group 'C' employee, other than a member of the Central Secretariat Clerical Service, works in a Ministry. No Schedule entry or Presidential order names a special authority. Who may impose a Rule 11 penalty?

  1. only the appointing authority, with no alternative route
  2. only the immediate head of the section where he works
  3. only the President, because Group 'C' penalties require higher sanction
  4. the Secretary to the Government of India in that Ministry, among the authorities Rule 12(3) names

Answer D. the Secretary to the Government of India in that Ministry, among the authorities Rule 12(3) names

Rule 12(3) enables the Secretary in the Ministry or Department, or the head of the office in other cases, to exercise the power subject to Rule 12(4).

Trap. Rule 12(3) is an enabling alternative; it does not oust the Rule 12(2) authority.

Decision 5Rule 12, Explanation II

An officer holding a Group 'C' post is promoted, on an officiating basis, to a Group 'B' post. A minor-penalty question arises. Explanation II to Rule 12 requires the file to treat him as:

  1. belonging to the higher Group for the purposes of that rule
  2. outside Rule 12 until the probation period ends
  3. belonging to both Groups, requiring both authorities' concurrence
  4. remaining in the lower Group until confirmation

Answer A. belonging to the higher Group for the purposes of that rule

Explanation II deems a Government servant promoted, whether on probation or temporarily, to belong to the higher Group for the purposes of Rule 12.

Trap. Officiating or probationary promotion already shifts the Group for competence.

Decision 6Rule 12(4)(b)

An officer substantively appointed to a General Central Service post is temporarily appointed to another Service. The authority in the temporary Service proposes a major penalty without consulting anyone. The defect is:

  1. failure to obtain the President's prior approval
  2. failure to consult the authority competent but for the temporary appointment
  3. no defect, because a temporary appointment transfers full competence
  4. failure to consult the lending State Government before the penalty

Answer B. failure to consult the authority competent but for the temporary appointment

Rule 12(4)(b) requires consultation with a non-subordinate authority that would have been competent but for the temporary appointment.

Trap. Temporary appointment does not erase the substantive service's competence interest.

Decision 7Rule 12(3)(b)

The head of an office is lower in rank than the authority competent under Rule 12(2). A censure order is signed by that head of office. The order is:

  1. competent, because censure is the lightest penalty
  2. competent, because the head of office is the immediate controlling authority for discipline
  3. not competent: Rule 12(3)(b) excludes a head of office junior to the Rule 12(2) authority
  4. valid if the employee consents to that forum in writing

Answer C. not competent: Rule 12(3)(b) excludes a head of office junior to the Rule 12(2) authority

Rule 12(3)(b) allows the head of an office to act only where he is not lower in rank than the authority competent under Rule 12(2).

Trap. Relative rank, not the lightness of the penalty, decides this competence.

Decision 8Rule 13(1)

A Ministry wants a subordinate authority to institute proceedings against an officer of a different cadre. There is no Presidential general or special order of empowerment. The lawful route is:

  1. institution by the Central Vigilance Commission
  2. institution by the officer's immediate superior in every case
  3. a Ministry-level note authorising any subordinate authority to institute
  4. institution by an authority competent under the rules, or a direction traceable to Rule 13(1)

Answer D. institution by an authority competent under the rules, or a direction traceable to Rule 13(1)

Rule 13(1) vests institution in the President or an authority empowered by him, and permits that authority to direct a competent disciplinary authority to institute proceedings.

Trap. Administrative convenience and vigilance involvement do not create institution competence.

Decision 9Rule 12(1); Rule 22(i)

The President personally imposes censure on a Group 'A' officer. The officer asks whether the penalty itself is incompetent. The correct advice is:

  1. the penalty is within Rule 12(1); what Rule 22(i) bars is an appeal against a Presidential order
  2. the penalty is void because censure always requires a Rule 14 inquiry
  3. the penalty is void unless a committee of secretaries concurs
  4. the penalty is void because the President may impose only major penalties

Answer A. the penalty is within Rule 12(1); what Rule 22(i) bars is an appeal against a Presidential order

Rule 12(1) lets the President impose any Rule 11 penalty on any Government servant; Rule 22(i) separately bars an appeal against an order made by the President.

Trap. Competence to punish and availability of an appeal are distinct questions.

Decision 10Rule 14(21)(a)-(b)

An inquiry held by an authority competent only to impose minor penalties finds that a major penalty is warranted. The AO must send the record to:

  1. the appellate authority, for a decision on quantum
  2. the disciplinary authority competent to impose the major penalty, which may act on the evidence or recall witnesses
  3. the President in every such case, because a major penalty always needs the highest sanction
  4. the Review Committee, for a recommendation on guilt

Answer B. the disciplinary authority competent to impose the major penalty, which may act on the evidence or recall witnesses

Rule 14(21) requires the record to be forwarded to the authority competent to impose the major penalty, which may act on the evidence or recall and examine witnesses in the interests of justice.

Trap. The forwarding authority records its finding; it does not impose the major penalty.

Decision 1Rule 10(1); Rule 12, GOI decision (1)

An officer holding only current charge of a post, not substantively appointed to it, signs a suspension order. The validity question turns on:

Suspension is a statutory power. The cited instruction states that officers performing the current duties of a post cannot exercise statutory powers under the rules.

Trap. Later approval does not cure an order made by someone who never held the power.

Decision 2Rule 10(5)(c)

A subordinate authority revokes a suspension ordered by its own superior. The revocation is:

  1. effective, because the disciplinary authority ratified the revocation later
  2. effective, because the Review Committee had recommended revocation
  3. effective, because a revocation always operates in the employee's favour
  4. ineffective, because only a higher authority in that line may revoke it

Rule 10(5)(c) confines modification or revocation to the authority which made or is deemed to have made the order, or an authority to which that authority is subordinate.

Trap. A favourable direction does not create competence to issue it.

Decision 3Rule 10(5)(b)

Suspension is continuing for one proceeding when a second disciplinary proceeding begins. To keep the officer under suspension until both end, the file needs:

  4. an automatic extension worked by the second charge-sheet

Answer A. a direction by the competent authority for reasons recorded in writing

Rule 10(5)(b) requires the authority competent to place the officer under suspension to direct continuation, for reasons recorded in writing, until all or any of the proceedings terminate.

Trap. The committee recommends; the competent authority directs.

Decision 4Rule 10(2)(b)

An officer is convicted and sentenced to two years' imprisonment; the department has not dismissed him. His status from the date of conviction is:

  1. ordinary duty until a formal suspension order is signed by the appointing authority

  3. automatic dismissal from the date of conviction by the trial court
  4. deemed suspension from the date of release from prison

Answer B. deemed suspension from the date of conviction, because no terminal penalty followed

Rule 10(2)(b) deems suspension from the date of conviction where the sentence exceeds 48 hours and dismissal, removal or compulsory retirement does not forthwith follow.

Trap. Conviction does not itself terminate service; the deeming provision fixes status and date.

Decision 5Rule 10(3)

A terminal penalty imposed on a suspended officer is set aside in appeal and the case is remitted for further inquiry. The officer claims he was never under suspension during the interval. Correct advice:

  1. suspension restarts only from the date of the appellate order of remission
  2. no suspension can exist once the original penalty has been set aside
  3. Rule 10(3) deems the suspension to continue from the date of the original order
  4. suspension continues only if a fresh suspension order is issued

Answer C. Rule 10(3) deems the suspension to continue from the date of the original order

Where a penalty of dismissal, removal or compulsory retirement is set aside in appeal or review and the case is remitted, Rule 10(3) deems the suspension to continue from the original order.

Trap. The deeming runs backwards to the date of the original penalty.

Decision 6Rule 10(4), proviso

A court sets aside removal on purely technical grounds without examining merits. The disciplinary authority wants a further inquiry. Under Rule 10(4) and its proviso:

  1. permissible without any assessment of the court's reasoning on the file
  2. permissible, but only with the leave of the court that set aside the removal order
  3. barred, because any judicial order setting aside the penalty forecloses further inquiry
  4. permissible only for a technical-ground decision, with suspension deemed from the original order

Answer D. permissible only for a technical-ground decision, with suspension deemed from the original order

The proviso confines the route to a decision passed purely on technical grounds without going into merits; the deeming runs from the original penalty order.

Trap. The nature of the judicial decision, not its mere existence, controls the route.

Decision 7FR 53(1)(ii)(a)(i)

After three months of suspension the competent authority finds the delay is not attributable to the officer. The subsistence component of Rs 20,000 may be increased by:

Case 63 · Drafting and instituting the charge-sheet

Decision 1Rule 14(3)(i)-(ii)

A draft charge memo narrates the conclusion ('misappropriated funds') without stating facts, documents or witnesses. The defect is that Rule 14(3) requires:

  1. definite and distinct articles, with imputations listing documents and witnesses
  2. the inquiry officer's tentative findings on each allegation framed
  3. a statement of the penalty proposed, inside the charge memo itself
  4. a single consolidated article that covers all the allegations together

Answer A. definite and distinct articles, with imputations listing documents and witnesses

Rule 14(3) requires the substance of the imputations to be drawn into definite and distinct articles of charge, supported by a statement of imputations with lists of documents and witnesses.

Trap. Conclusions are not charges; the factual basis and the evidence lists are mandatory.

Decision 2Rule 14(3)(i)

Two unrelated incidents from different years are merged into one article of charge. The AO should:

  1. keep them merged, to save the inquiry time and paper
  2. split them into separate articles that can each be answered distinctly
  3. leave the framing of the articles entirely to the presenting officer
  4. drop the older incident without examining it on the record

Answer B. split them into separate articles that can each be answered distinctly

Distinctness in the articles is what allows each imputation to be separately stated, defended and found.

Trap. Clubbing unrelated allegations prejudices a clear defence and a reasoned finding.

Decision 3Rule 14(4); cited instructions

A charge memo requires a written statement of defence within three days and omits any reference to being heard in person. Under Rule 14(4) read with the cited instructions, the AO should:

  1. omit the personal-hearing question, since the defence is in writing
  2. keep the three days, because the rule itself fixes no period
  3. require a reasonable period, normally fifteen days, and ask about being heard in person
  4. require the written defence to be filed only at the first hearing

Answer C. require a reasonable period, normally fifteen days, and ask about being heard in person

Rule 14(4) requires delivery of the charge, imputations and lists, with a specified period for the defence and a query whether the officer desires to be heard in person; the standard instruction is fifteen days.

Trap. The rule's silence on the exact period does not licence an unreasonably short one.

Decision 4Rule 14(6)

An inquiry officer starts the inquiry without receiving the written statement of defence, the witness statements or the order appointing the presenting officer. Which duty was skipped?

  1. the Review Committee's duty to brief the inquiry officer on the file
  2. the appellate authority's duty to transmit the original record
  3. the employee's duty to serve copies on the presenting officer directly
  4. the disciplinary authority's duty under Rule 14(6) to forward the listed papers

Answer D. the disciplinary authority's duty under Rule 14(6) to forward the listed papers

Rule 14(6) lists the charge, imputations, defence statement, witness statements, proof of delivery of documents and the presenting officer's appointment order as the papers to be forwarded.

Trap. The inquiry begins on the disciplinary authority's complete reference, not on the charge memo alone.

Decision 5Rule 14(7)

The inquiry officer fixes the first appearance eleven working days after receipt of the reference and refuses any extension. The rule position is:

  1. appearance within ten working days, extendable by not more than ten days
  2. no extension of the first appearance is ever permissible at all
  3. appearance may be fixed within thirty days in every case
  4. any period the inquiry officer prefers is lawful on the facts

Answer A. appearance within ten working days, extendable by not more than ten days

Rule 14(7) fixes appearance within ten working days from receipt by the inquiring authority, with further time not exceeding ten days.

Trap. The outer date of the first appearance is a rule-based limit, not a matter of convenience.

Decision 6Rule 16(1)(a)

A censure proposal rests on a note that the officer 'has been careless'. No written intimation of the proposal or the imputations was given, though the file records that his explanation was 'not called for'. Under Rule 16(1)(a):

  2. the procedure is defective for want of written intimation and a representation opportunity

  4. the procedure is complete, because the officer had signed the file earlier

Answer B. the procedure is defective for want of written intimation and a representation opportunity

Rule 16(1)(a) requires written information of the proposal and the imputations and a reasonable opportunity of representation before a minor penalty is imposed.

Trap. Minor penalties need a shorter procedure, not the absence of one.

Decision 7Rule 16(1-A)

The competent authority proposes to withhold increments for five years. The AO must advise that:

  1. no inquiry is needed for any withholding of increments at all
  2. the Rule 16 written procedure alone is sufficient in this case
  3. a Rule 14 inquiry is mandatory, because the withholding exceeds three years
  4. only withholding with cumulative effect needs a regular inquiry

Answer C. a Rule 14 inquiry is mandatory, because the withholding exceeds three years

Rule 16(1-A) requires a regular inquiry where the withholding is likely to affect pension adversely, exceeds three years, or is with cumulative effect.

Trap. Three separate triggers exist; the three-year period is only one of them.

Decision 8Proviso to Rule 14(2); Rule 3C, CCS (Conduct) Rules

A complaint of sexual harassment under the Conduct Rules is received, and the AO appoints a deputy secretary as inquiry officer. Under the proviso to Rule 14(2):

  1. the case must be handled only under the Rule 19 route
  2. the complaint must first be referred to the police in every case
  3. any officer of deputy secretary rank may be appointed to inquire
  4. the Complaints Committee is deemed the inquiring authority in such a case

Answer D. the Complaints Committee is deemed the inquiring authority in such a case

The proviso deems the Complaints Committee established for such complaints to be the inquiring authority, holding the inquiry so far as practicable under the rules where no separate procedure applies.

Trap. A deeming provision is not displaced by appointing a convenient substitute officer.

Decision 9Rule 14(1)

A removal order records that the officer was 'heard at length by the Secretary', but no inquiry was held and Rule 19 is not invoked. The order is:

  1. unsustainable, because a major penalty needs an inquiry under Rule 14 and 15
  2. sustainable only if the Secretary happens to be the appointing authority

  1. the period runs from his first representation against the order
  2. the appeal period runs from the date printed on the order and not from its delivery to him
  3. the forty-five day period runs from delivery, which the record cannot establish
  4. the appeal period runs from the date the file was closed

Answer C. the forty-five day period runs from delivery, which the record cannot establish

Rule 25 fixes forty-five days from the date on which a copy of the order is delivered, so the delivery date is a record requirement in practice.

Trap. An unrecorded delivery date converts every later limitation dispute into an oral argument.

Decision 6Rule 26(3)

An appellate record is called for and the office sends only the penalty order, keeping the inquiry papers back. The defect is that:

  1. the record may be sent only when the appellate authority repeats the request
  2. only the order is relevant to an appeal against it
  3. the inquiry papers are privileged from the appellate authority
  4. the appeal must go forward with the relevant record, without waiting for a direction

Answer D. the appeal must go forward with the relevant record, without waiting for a direction

Rule 26(3) requires the appeal and the relevant record to be forwarded without avoidable delay.

Trap. An appellate authority cannot test procedure and evidence on the order alone.

Decision 7Rules 30, 31, 33–34

A file is being closed under Part IX provisions: notice service, a missed procedural time limit and an old saving question. Which provisions govern?

  1. Rule 30 for service, Rule 31 for relaxation of time, and Rules 33–34 for saving
  2. Rule 22 for exclusions from the service, Rule 27 for disposal of appeals and Rule 29 for revision
  3. Rule 11 for penalties and Rule 16 for the procedure
  4. Rule 14 for the inquiry and Rule 18 for common proceedings

Answer A. Rule 30 for service, Rule 31 for relaxation of time, and Rules 33–34 for saving

Part IX (Miscellaneous) carries service, relaxation of time, transitory provisions and repeal/saving; the other choices name provisions from other Parts.

Trap. Naming the Part correctly is the quickest way to reach the right rule.

Decision 8Rule 18(2); Rule 15

A Rule 18 common proceeding ends with different penalties for different officers. The record must contain:

  1. only the penalty of the senior-most officer in the proceeding
  2. individual findings and penalty for each officer, as the order specifies
  3. the disciplinary authority's note, without separate findings for each
  4. a single consolidated finding covering every officer in the case

Answer B. individual findings and penalty for each officer, as the order specifies

The Rule 18(2) order specifies the authority, the penalties and the procedure; the proceeding then produces individual findings and orders for each officer.

Trap. A common proceeding shares the record, not the outcome.

Decision 9Rule 17; access-to-record rules

An officer asks for copies of the whole inquiry record after the final order. The AO's correct approach is to:

  1. refer the request to the appellate authority for orders on the file
  2. refuse every request once the case has been finally closed at the desk
  3. supply the required findings and reasons; treat wider requests under the access rules
  4. send the entire file to the officer at once, without any assessment of what is protected

Answer C. supply the required findings and reasons; treat wider requests under the access rules

Rule 17 requires the findings and disagreement reasons to be supplied with the order; a wider request is tested under the applicable access rules rather than granted or refused wholesale.

Trap. There are two steps: what the rules require to be given, and what a wider request may attract.

Decision 10Rules 14–17; Rule 30

A disciplinary file is reconstructed after the original papers were lost. The AO should record that:

  1. the penalty stands even if the record cannot be reconstructed at all, because the order is final
  2. reconstruction is unnecessary because the order is on the register
  3. a fresh inquiry is the only permissible course in every such case
  4. reconstruction must be from authenticated copies, with verification preserved

Answer D. reconstruction must be from authenticated copies, with verification preserved

A reconstructed record is only as good as its authentication, and the officer's opportunity to deal with it must be preserved before any further order rests on it.

Trap. A register entry proves that an order exists; it does not prove what the inquiry found.

Case 74 · Pay, duty status and leave after a CCA order

Decision 1FR 54-B; reinstatement instructions

A suspension is revoked and the period is regularised as duty on the footing that the suspension was wholly unjustified. Full pay follows because:

  1. the consequence follows a finding that the suspension was wholly unjustified
  2. revocation of a suspension always carries full pay as a matter of course
  3. the officer's representation asking for full pay settles the question
  4. the appellate authority's sympathy supplies the entitlement to full pay

Answer A. the consequence follows a finding that the suspension was wholly unjustified

The regularisation of a suspension period distinguishes a wholly unjustified suspension from other cases, and the pay consequence follows the recorded finding.

Trap. Revocation ends the suspension; it does not by itself decide whether it was unjustified.

Decision 2FR 54; FR 54-B

A suspension is revoked, the authority records that it was not wholly unjustified, and the officer is treated as on duty for the period. His pay for that period is regulated by:

  1. no pay, because the suspension was not wholly unjustified
  2. the rule for a period treated as duty, on the competent authority's order
  3. the appellate authority's discretion alone, on a representation made by the officer
  4. full pay in every case, because he was treated as on duty

Answer B. the rule for a period treated as duty, on the competent authority's order

The treatment of the period and the pay for it both follow the competent authority's regularisation order, which FR 54 and FR 54-B regulate.

Trap. Duty status and the rate of pay are decided together, not separately by impression.

Decision 3Rule 27(3); Rule 28

An appellate order sets aside a penalty and directs that the period between dismissal and reinstatement be treated as duty. The AO must:

  1. await a separate representation from the officer before acting
  2. leave the period's treatment to a fresh disciplinary order
  3. give effect to that direction in the pay and service record, since it is part of the order
  4. treat the period as extraordinary leave, because the dismissal was in force throughout that period

Answer C. give effect to that direction in the pay and service record, since it is part of the order

Where the appellate order itself fixes the treatment of the period, that direction is part of the order to be implemented, and the pay follows it.

Trap. A specific direction in the appellate order does not need to be reinvented at the desk.

Decision 4Rule 23(v)(f)

An order holds that a suspension period shall not count as duty for a specified purpose. The officer's remedy is:

  1. no remedy at all, because only penalties are appealable and an order on counting is not one
  2. a revision only, since an order on counting is not appealable
  3. a representation to the inquiry officer who handled the case
  4. an appeal against that order under the Rule 23 clause on counting of suspension periods

Answer D. an appeal against that order under the Rule 23 clause on counting of suspension periods

Rule 23 makes specified non-penal orders appealable, including an order on whether a suspension period counts as duty for a stated purpose.

Trap. Non-penal orders sit in their own appeal clauses; they are not outside Part VII.

Decision 5Rule 23(v)(e)(ii)

An order fixes the officer's pay for the interval between dismissal and reinstatement. He disputes the figure. The appeal position is that:

  1. the order is appealable under the Rule 23 clause on pay for the interval
  2. the figure can be challenged only by a writ petition, since a pay fixation is not appealable
  3. the figure is final because pay is a finance subject
  4. the figure can be revised only by the pay commission

Answer A. the order is appealable under the Rule 23 clause on pay for the interval

Rule 23(v)(e)(ii) specifically addresses an order fixing pay for the interval between dismissal and reinstatement.

Trap. A linked order has its own appeal clause, which must be identified before filing.

Decision 6Rule 23(v)(d)

The officer disputes only the subsistence allowance, not the suspension order. The appeal lies under:

  1. no clause, because subsistence is not covered by the rules
  2. the Rule 23 clause on subsistence, apart from the suspension appeal
  3. Rule 27(2), because it is in the nature of a penalty appeal
  4. Rule 23(i), because the matter concerns a suspension

Answer B. the Rule 23 clause on subsistence, apart from the suspension appeal

Rule 23(v)(d) treats an order on subsistence allowance as its own appealable category, distinct from the suspension order itself.

Trap. Suspension and subsistence are separate appealable orders with separate clauses.

Decision 7Rule 28

A penalty of reduction is set aside but consequential recovery already made is not refunded. The AO should record that:

  1. the officer must file a separate civil suit to recover it
  2. recoveries once made are never refunded to a servant
  3. implementing the appellate order includes restoring what was recovered under it
  4. the refund needs a fresh sanction from the appellate authority before any amount is restored

Answer C. implementing the appellate order includes restoring what was recovered under it

Rule 28 requires the order-making authority to implement the appellate order; leaving its monetary consequence unaddressed is non-implementation.

Trap. An order that is set aside must be undone in the accounts as well as on the record.

Decision 8FR 54-B; leave rules as applied to suspension periods

An officer is reinstated after a set-aside and claims leave for the suspension period that he never applied for. The AO should record that:

  1. leave must be granted for the whole period since he was not on duty
  2. no leave can ever be granted for any period of suspension
  3. the period is automatically treated as extraordinary leave
  4. leave needs an application; the period follows the regularisation order

Answer D. leave needs an application; the period follows the regularisation order

The regularisation rules, not an unapplied leave request, decide how a suspension period is treated; leave is a separate application-based entitlement.

Trap. Ordering the consequences requires the regularisation decision first.

Decision 9Rule 28; Rule 11(iv)

A penalty of withholding of increments is set aside. The officer's increments were withheld for two years and his pay stagnated. The AO must:

  1. restore the increments from the date of the set-aside order and correct the pay drawn
  2. restore the increments only from the date of his next promotion
  3. treat the withheld increments as a permanent saving to the department
  4. wait for the next pay revision before correcting the record of the increments at all

Answer A. restore the increments from the date of the set-aside order and correct the pay drawn

Implementation restores what the set-aside penalty withheld; the pay and the record must both reflect the position as it stands after the appellate order.

Trap. A penalty undone on paper but not on the pay bill is only half undone.

Decision 10FR 54; Rule 28

An officer is exonerated and seeks compensation for the period of suspension. The AO's correct position is that:

  1. compensation is payable because the inquiry against him failed
  2. the disciplinary rules provide pay and treatment of the period, not compensation
  3. compensation is payable on the disciplinary authority's own motion
  4. full pay is the same thing as compensation and should be paid to him as such on exoneration

Answer B. the disciplinary rules provide pay and treatment of the period, not compensation

The framework addresses pay, duty status and regularisation of the period; a claim for compensation is not a disciplinary remedy and is not created by exoneration.

Trap. Distinguish what the rules give from what a court may separately award.

Case 75 · Pension consequences of a CCA penalty

Decision 1Rule 28; pension rules

A major penalty under Rule 11 is set aside on appeal after the officer has retired. The pension sanctioning authority asks what effect the set-aside has. The AO should record that:

  1. the pension must be recalculated only by the appellate authority
  2. the pension record is untouched because pension is a separate subject
  3. the penalty has fallen, so the pension record must be corrected accordingly
  4. the set-aside reduces pension permanently as part of a compromise

Answer C. the penalty has fallen, so the pension record must be corrected accordingly

A set-aside removes the penalty and its consequences, including its effect on the service record that pension is computed from, so the record must be corrected.

Trap. Pension follows the corrected service record; it is not insulated from a disciplinary set-aside.

Decision 2Rule 28; pension rules

A penalty of dismissal is set aside and the officer is reinstated after the date of his normal superannuation. The AO should record that:

  1. the officer cannot be reinstated because he has crossed superannuation
  2. full pension accrues automatically and requires no further order
  3. the case must await a fresh inquiry before any pension is settled
  4. reinstatement restores the record; pension follows the treatment directed

Answer D. reinstatement restores the record; pension follows the treatment directed

Reinstatement and the treatment of the intervening period are governed by the appellate order and the regularisation rules; pension then follows that record.

Trap. Superannuation does not make a set-aside order inoperative.

Decision 3Rule 11; pension rules

A minor penalty of withholding of increments was in force during the last year of service. The retirement papers must record that:

  1. the operative penalty, its currency and its effect on increments must be shown
  2. the penalty lapses on retirement and need not be mentioned at all
  3. the penalty converts into a permanent reduction at retirement
  4. the penalty is irrelevant because pension is computed on average emoluments of the last ten months

Answer A. the operative penalty, its currency and its effect on increments must be shown

The record at retirement must show the penalties in force and their effect, because pension is computed from the corrected emoluments and the service record.

Trap. A penalty that has monetary effect must appear where the money is computed.

Decision 4Rules 14–15; pension rules on proceedings continuing after retirement

A departmental proceeding instituted before retirement continues after it. Under the CCS (CCA) framework the proceeding:

  1. continues only with the officer's written consent to it
  2. continues, and the finding goes to the competent pension authority
  3. converts automatically into a pension proceeding under Rule 29-A
  4. abates, because the officer is no longer a Government servant

Answer B. continues, and the finding goes to the competent pension authority

Proceedings instituted while in service continue under the CCS (CCA) framework; the finding is then dealt with by the pension authority under the pension rules.

Trap. The CCA proceeding does not become something else because service has ended.

Decision 5Rule 28; FR 54-B; pension rules

An officer retires while a suspension is in force and the suspension is later revoked with the period treated as duty. The pension papers should reflect:

  1. the period can be counted only if the appellate authority directs
  2. the suspension period is deducted from qualifying service always
  3. the treatment of the period as directed, and the pay and service record that follow
  4. the period counts automatically as duty without any order at all, once the suspension is revoked

Answer C. the treatment of the period as directed, and the pay and service record that follow

Qualifying service is computed from the service record as corrected by the regularisation decision and any appellate direction.

Trap. The pension computation cannot be better than the record it is fed.

Decision 6Rule 28; pension rules on proceedings after retirement

A penalty order is set aside because the inquiry was vitiated, and the department proposes to proceed afresh against a retired officer. The first question on the file is:

  1. whether the officer will cooperate with the fresh inquiry at all
  2. whether the appellate authority will permit a fresh inquiry
  3. whether the inquiry officer who conducted the earlier proceeding is still available to the office
  4. whether a fresh proceeding may lawfully be instituted, the pension rules governing

Answer D. whether a fresh proceeding may lawfully be instituted, the pension rules governing

A fresh proceeding against a retired person raises a threshold competence question under the pension framework before any merits are reached.

Trap. Competence comes before convenience in every post-retirement file.

Decision 7Rule 28; service-record rules

The pension papers show a Rule 11 penalty that was later substituted on appeal by a lighter penalty. The AO must ensure that:

  1. the substituted penalty is recorded, with the earlier entry marked as modified on appeal
  2. both entries are deleted and no penalty at all is shown, because substitution wipes the original
  3. the heavier penalty stays, because it was the one originally imposed
  4. the file notes the change but the register stays as it was

Answer A. the substituted penalty is recorded, with the earlier entry marked as modified on appeal

Implementation of the appellate order requires the register to reflect the operative, substituted penalty rather than the superseded one.

Trap. A record that hides the appellate history invites the same objection all over again.

Decision 8Rule 17; Rule 30

An officer's pension is to be computed and the file shows a censure from six years earlier that was never communicated to him. The AO should record that:

  1. the censure has lapsed by efflux of time without any order being needed to efface it
  2. an uncommunicated penalty cannot be treated as operative until the defect is cured
  3. the entry is irrelevant to pension in every case without exception
  4. the censure stands because it appears on the service register

Answer B. an uncommunicated penalty cannot be treated as operative until the defect is cured

Rule 17 requires the order to be communicated with the findings, and Rule 30 governs service; an uncommunicated order is defective on the face of the record.

Trap. A register entry without communication is an unfinished order.

Decision 9Rules 24–28; pension rules

A retired officer's disciplinary file shows an appeal that was filed but never decided. The pension papers cannot be finalised. The AO should record that:

  1. the file may be closed with a note that the appeal is pending before the appellate authority
  2. the appeal lapses on retirement, leaving the penalty final
  3. the undecided appeal must be disposed of before the penalty is treated as final
  4. the pension authority may decide the appeal itself on the file

Answer C. the undecided appeal must be disposed of before the penalty is treated as final

An undecided appeal leaves the operative penalty unsettled, and pension computed on an unsettled penalty will not be defensible.

Trap. Finality of the penalty is a precondition to finality of the benefit.

Decision 10Rule 28

A copy of the appellate order reducing a penalty reaches the pension office, but the service book still carries the original penalty. The single defect the AO must fix is that:

  1. the appellate order was not countersigned by the pension office
  2. the pension office should have waited for the disciplinary authority
  3. the officer should have applied for the correction himself
  4. the service book was not corrected to reflect the operative penalty

Answer D. the service book was not corrected to reflect the operative penalty

Implementation is a duty of the order-making authority, and it runs to correcting the records on which later benefits are computed.

Trap. An appellate order implemented only in the file and not in the register is not implemented.
