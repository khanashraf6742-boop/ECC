### [ch03-005] card L984-1008 Very High-Yield Daily Allowance on Tour — Disturbances, Curfews, Sundays, and Holiday Rules (SR 51, SR 72, SR 73)

Very High-Yield Daily Allowance on Tour — Disturbances, Curfews, Sundays, and Holiday Rules (SR 51, SR 72, SR 73)

Exact-Source Fact Card
Under Swamy's Compilation of FR & SR (Part-II, Travelling Allowances) and GIOs under SR 51, SR 72, and SR 73 , the admissibility of Daily Allowance (DA) during extraordinary situations and halts on tour is strictly regulated:


1. DA during Disturbances/Imposition of Curfew (GIO (4)(b), SR 51):
If an employee's stay on tour is involuntarily extended due to natural disasters, civil disturbances, blockades, or the imposition of a curfew, and their absence is regularized as Special Casual Leave (SCL) , Daily Allowance (Food & Hotel) is fully admissible for the entire period of such Special Casual Leave.

2. DA for Sundays and Holidays (GIO (1), SR 72):
Daily Allowance is admissible for Sundays and public holidays falling during a tour ONLY if the employee is actually on camp (at the tour station) on those days and not merely constructively on camp. No DA is allowed if the employee leaves the tour station to visit their family or headquarters on weekends.

3. DA during Leave/Restricted Holidays (GIO (1), SR 72):
NO Daily Allowance is admissible for any kind of leave (Casual Leave, Earned Leave, etc.) or Restricted Holidays (RH) availed of by the employee while on tour.

4. The 180-Day Continuous Halt Limit (GIO (1), SR 73):
A 180-day continuous-halt limit appears in the referenced secondary material, but the current primary text and its application to a particular training course have not been verified. Do not use this general tour note to decide training DA; see the source-status card and check the current SR/GIO and facility terms.

### [ch03-008] card L1069-1091 High-Yield Timing rules — how much DA for how long you were away

High-Yield Timing rules — how much DA for how long you were away

Exact-Source Fact Card
Absence from headquarters is reckoned on a midnight-to-midnight (calendar day) basis under the 13.07.2017 O.M. The tour food-DA scale is <6 hours: 30%; 6–12 hours: 70%; >12 hours: 100%. The signed DoE letter No. 904302/2018-E.IV dated 12.09.2018 states that, for qualifying local journeys (temporary duty point beyond 8 km from normal place of duty), local food DA is 50% of the tour amount: 15% / 35% / 50%. The complete current SR 71/GIO and at/below-8-km treatment remain unverified.




Length of absence from HQ | % of lump-sum DA payable |

Less than 6 hours | 30% |

6 to 12 hours | 70% |

More than 12 hours | 100% |


Trap note: keep the 30/70/100% tour-food-DA scale separate from the signed 12.09.2018 DoE local-journey clarification, which states 15/35/50% of the tour amount for journeys beyond 8 km from the normal place of duty. The full current SR 71/GIO text remains open.

Worked Example
Scenario: An officer in Level 9 tours from 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM the same day (7 hours), headquartered in a non-X city.
Given: Level 9 lump-sum food DA = ₹1,125/day (current rate).
Step 1: Absence duration = 7 hours → falls in the "6–12 hours" band.
Step 2: Applicable percentage = 70%.
Step 3: DA payable = 70% × ₹1,125 = ₹787.50 .
Result: ₹787.50 is the food-component lump sum; hotel/travel components are claimed separately if actually incurred.
Counter-example: If the same officer's tour lasted 5 hours 45 minutes instead, they'd drop into the "<6 hours" band and receive only 30% (₹337.50) — a 40-percentage-point cliff for a 15-minute difference. This cliff-edge is a favourite trap in exams.

### [ch03-010] card L1106-1126 High-Yield Split Duty & Combined Absence — DA Calculation Rule

High-Yield Split Duty & Combined Absence — DA Calculation Rule

Exact-Source Fact Card
Under the TA Rules, Daily Allowance is calculated on a calendar-day (midnight-to-midnight) basis . This means that if an official is required to leave headquarters for a task, return mid-day, and then go out again the same day (a "split duty" scenario), the total absence from headquarters within that single calendar day is aggregated for DA calculation:


Split Duty Example: An official leaves HQ at 9:00 AM, returns at 1:00 PM (4 hours absent), then goes out again at 3:00 PM and returns at 7:00 PM (4 hours absent). Total absence on that calendar day = 4 + 4 = 8 hours . This falls in the 6–12 hours band → 70% DA is payable.

Key Principle: The periods are not treated as two separate journeys each attracting 30% DA. Instead, the combined total absence determines the applicable percentage band for the entire day.

Local-journey scope: The signed 12.09.2018 letter gives the 15/35/50 food-DA scale for qualifying local journeys (temporary duty point beyond 8 km from normal place of duty). It does not address split-trip aggregation; do not extend the ordinary-tour aggregation statement to local journeys without the controlling current SR/GIO.

### [ch03-011] micronote L1127-1136 

Transit DA for Overnight Journeys — Calendar-Day Calculation

When a train or flight spans across midnight, the DA for each calendar day is calculated separately based on the actual hours of absence on that specific day. This is not a "full day" just because you were travelling.

Worked Example: A Level 9 officer (Food DA ceiling: ₹1,125/day) boards a train departing at 8:00 AM on Day 1 and arrives at the destination at 2:00 AM on Day 2 .

• Day 1: Absence from 8:00 AM to midnight (24:00) = 16 hours → >12 hours band → 100% DA = ₹1,125.
• Day 2: Absence from midnight (00:00) to 2:00 AM = 2 hours → <6 hours band → 30% DA = ₹337.50.
• Total DA for this transit: ₹1,125 + ₹337.50 = ₹1,462.50 .

Key Insight: Day 2's absence is only 2 hours (midnight to 2 AM), NOT a "full travel day." Many candidates wrongly claim 100% DA for both days — a classic trap!





### [ch03-012] micronote L1137-1145 

Duty Pass vs TA — Railway Employees' Special Rule

Indian Railway employees are entitled to Duty Passes (free rail travel on official duty) issued under Railway Board rules. This is a separate entitlement from the Travelling Allowance (TA) system covered in this guide.

• If a Railway employee uses a Duty Pass for a journey, they cannot simultaneously claim TA (travel fare) for that same leg of the journey.
• If a Railway employee is eligible for a Duty Pass but does not use it (e.g., travels by air because rail is not feasible), they cannot claim TA for that leg unless a specific relaxation is granted by the competent authority.
• Non-Railway Ministries: Employees of all other Central Government Ministries/Departments have no Duty Pass system — they claim TA as per the rules in this guide.

Exam Trap: "A Railway employee eligible for a Duty Pass chooses to travel by air on personal expense and claims TA." → Not admissible without specific relaxation.
