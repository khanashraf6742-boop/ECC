4. Which rule applies? Claim-classification decision tree

OrderProviso
Source L4222–4253

Follow the branches in order. Classify the purpose first; mode of transport, advance status, and pay level come later.

  1. Is this travel for official duty or a personal LTC journey?
    • Official duty, transfer, retirement, training, or a special family case: go to Step 2 and use the applicable FR/SR/DoE TA order.
    • Personal leave travel under LTC: go to Step 3 and use the CCS (LTC) Rules and separate DoPT LTC orders. Do not treat LTC as official-tour TA.
    • Defence / CAPF / another separately governed service: stop and identify the current service-specific/PCDA authority; this civilian guide may not govern.
  2. If official TA, which subcategory?
    • Tour / transfer / ordinary training: identify the current DoE order, pay/mode entitlement, journey and filing trigger. Ordinary claim submission is 60 days whether or not an advance was drawn. Check advance adjustment separately. If air travel is claimed, apply the ATA instruction within its air-ticket scope; rail booking follows the relevant rail rule.
    • Retirement TA: use the separate 180-day order and its conditions; do not use the ordinary 60-day line.
    • Training TA/DA: identify the actual course sanction and facility terms. Current SR 164/GIO schedules and station-sequence claims remain pending [D]; DoE O.M. 01.02.2018 separately addresses free boarding/lodging. Do not apply a universal reset or 30/150 formula.
    • Death-in-service family TA: use the SR 148 family-specific eligibility, advance, journey and adjustment provisions; do not import the ordinary tour-advance clock.
    • Technical resignation / Joining Time / TTA after appointment: compare G.S.R. 229(E) dated 27.03.2015 with DoPT O.M. dated 24.11.2022, para 3.6.3. The interaction remains unresolved here: get a written decision; do not infer entitlement from resignation status alone.
  3. If LTC, what is the mode and advance status?
    • Rail: use the CCS (LTC) rail entitlement and booking rules. The air ATA requirement is not a blanket rule for all rail tickets; eligible LTC e-ticketing through Indian Railways is addressed separately in the DoPT FAQ.
    • Air: check the employee’s LTC air entitlement and the applicable special dispensation. Use the prescribed ATA channel under the cited LTC-air orders. A 21-day lead is encouragement; the 30-day advance-request instruction is only for employees entitled to air travel.
    • Claim after return: determine whether an LTC advance was drawn: normally one month with advance / three months without, subject to the limited FAQ-described relaxation. Do not convert these to fixed day counts.
  4. Separate the four clocks: (1) claim submission, (2) advance adjustment, (3) request to draw an advance, and (4) recommended booking lead time. Write the event, source paragraph, responsible authority, and date for each. If a current signed authority cannot be located, mark the point UNRESOLVED and ask the competent office in writing.
Decision boundary: This tree directs the reader to the rule set; it does not decide an individual’s entitlement or replace sanction, audit, or a competent-authority ruling.

5. Practical claim-preparation checklist

Exception
Source L4254–4276

Use this as a file-completeness aid—not as a guarantee that a claim is admissible.

Before travel or transfer

  • □ Classify the journey first: official tour, transfer, retirement, training, LTC, or another category. Do not import an LTC condition into a TA claim (or vice versa).
  • □ Locate the current primary order for the category and record its number, date, paragraph, effective date, and any later amendment.
  • □ Keep written sanction/approval, duty dates, destination, route, purpose, pay level, and the authority approving the journey.
  • □ Check the applicable mode/class and booking instructions for this journey type. Do not rely on the unverified July 2026 LTC advisory.
  • □ If an advance is requested, obtain the governing current ceiling, adjustment trigger/date, and recovery terms from the competent office in writing. Do not assume a general 90% cap or a general 30-day transfer deadline.

When preparing the claim

  • □ Separate fare, Daily Allowance, hotel, local conveyance, CTG, baggage, and conveyance claims; apply each component’s own conditions and documentary requirements.
  • □ Keep tickets/boarding passes, receipts or certificates where required, route evidence, sanction, dates of departure/return, and proof of any permitted exception.
  • □ Use the ordinary 60-day or retirement 180-day claim window only for the relevant category and from the correct trigger date. Do not treat either as an advance-adjustment deadline.
  • □ For transfer claims, document station/residence changes and the basis for CTG, family travel, baggage, and vehicle transport; check current limits and ownership requirements.
  • □ If technical resignation is involved, decide that status separately from TTA and Joining Time Pay. For the 2015/2022 interaction, attach both authorities and request a written ruling.
  • □ Keep a copy of the submitted claim, acknowledgement, calculation sheet, sanctions, and any clarification or relaxation.
Stop-and-check cases: missing primary authority; a deadline inferred from an old compendium; a claim relying on the unlocated LTC advisory; or a technical-resignation case where Rule 4(4) / para 3.6.3 could affect TTA or Joining Time Pay. Escalate rather than guessing.

6. Worked calculations—with assumptions stated

Proviso
Source L4277–4283

Example A — double Transport Allowance arithmetic (not an eligibility ruling). Assume the applicable higher-TPTA-city base amount is ₹7,200 per month, the employee meets the separate disability-related double-allowance conditions, and DA is 63% for the relevant period. Calculation before payroll rounding: ₹7,200 × 2 × (1 + 0.63) = ₹23,472 per month. The regular-rate arithmetic is ₹7,200 × 1.63 = ₹11,736. Confirm the employee category, city, base rate, effective date, and payroll rounding under the current order.

Example B — CTG arithmetic (conditions must be checked). If the normal CTG rate is 80% and basic pay is ₹60,000, then ₹60,000 × 0.80 = ₹48,000. If the applicable same-station / residence-change rule limits CTG to one-third of the normal amount, then ₹48,000 ÷ 3 = ₹16,000. This is arithmetic only: verify the transfer category, distance, actual residence change, and governing order before treating either amount as payable.

Example C — Daily Allowance percentage arithmetic. Using the guide’s displayed Level 9–11 Food DA ceiling of ₹1,125, a valid 6–12-hour tour absence at 70% gives ₹1,125 × 0.70 = ₹787.50. If a distinct local-journey rule applies a 50% factor to that component, the arithmetic is ₹1,125 × 0.70 × 0.50 = ₹393.75. Confirm the journey category, time band, applicable table, and rounding; do not combine these factors unless the rule applies.

TermMeaning / caution
TATravelling Allowance; a family of entitlements, not one flat payment.

Claim-submission limitTime to submit a claim after the journey, under the applicable rule.
Advance adjustmentSeparate accounting/settlement obligation after an advance. Do not infer it from the claim-submission limit.

Primary vs secondary sourceA signed Government rule/order is primary authority. A handbook, compilation, or summary is a research aid unless its legal status and currency are established.
