⚠ Scope Disclaimer — Civilian vs Defence Personnel. This guide covers Civilian Central Government Ministries/Departments governed by the Department of Expenditure (DoE) O.M. No.19030/1/2017-E.IV. Defence Personnel (Army, Navy, Air Force) and personnel of CISF, Assam Rifles, and other Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs) are governed by separate orders issued by the PCDA (Principal Controller of Defence Accounts), Pune — not by the civilian DoE O.M. While the broad rationale (Pay-Level-based entitlements, Dearness-Allowance-linked rate revisions, CTG structure) is similar, the specific rates, class entitlements, and Road Mileage Allowance (RMA) tables may differ in Defence orders. For competitive exams like CDS, AFCAT, or NDA that test Defence-specific TA, always refer to the latest PCDA(O) Pune circulars. This guide's RMA state-wise table (Chapter 4) is included only as an illustration of the "state-specific rate" concept — it does not constitute the governing rate for civilian employees.

Route 1 · Exam study

Understand → retrieve → apply

  1. Chapters 1–2: build the rule framework and travel-entitlement map.
  2. Chapters 3–4: study tour/DA and mileage, then work the examples.
  3. Chapters 5–6: study transfer and retirement components, keeping their separate CTG and deadline rules apart.
  4. Chapters 7–8: use Chapter 7 as a selected historical amendment chronology, then study the current-rate mechanism; do not treat every old entry as current.
  5. Chapters 9–11: practise calculations, definitions, deadlines and exceptions; keep source holds visible.
  6. Chapters 12–17: use the technical-resignation, joining-time, case-study, comparison, MCQ and FAQ material as a specialist extension.
  7. Finish with Appendices A–D: use flashcards and reference indexes for retrieval; keep unverified MCQs unscored, then consult the source-check log and optional claim toolkit.

Classify → verify → calculate → document

  1. Classify the journey: identify the claim type and the facts that trigger the rule.

How to use repeated summaries and historical material

Fact-sheets, rapid-revision recaps, flashcards and the AO quick-reference sheet intentionally repeat selected rules for recall. They are summaries, not separate authority; if a number or shorthand differs, return to the full chapter and the source-status entry. Chapter 7 is a selected historical chronology, not a current-rule list. Check issue date, effective date, scope and later amendments before using an old O.M.

Use the same loop in every chapter

  1. State the rule: identify the trigger, scope, exception and effective date.
  2. Check the source status: distinguish a primary-source finding from a secondary lead or an unresolved point.
  3. Apply it: work the illustration or case without looking at the explanation.
  4. Retrieve it: close the section, answer its questions, then check the source. Mark each point know / unsure / source check and revisit it the next day and again about a week later; never turn “unsure” into an assumed rule.
